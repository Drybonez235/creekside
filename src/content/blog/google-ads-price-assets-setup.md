# How to Add Price Assets to Google Ads (Including the Three-Asset Rule Most Advertisers Don't Know)

This post is based on a video Peterson published on the Creekside Marketing YouTube channel: [Adding Price Assets to Your Google Ads Campaign](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=soIdSaLVsEE).

Google Ads price assets are one of the most overlooked features in paid search. They put your actual pricing, service packages, or product tiers directly into search results, giving potential buyers meaningful information before they click. Most advertisers skip them because the setup path is buried in the interface and the three-asset minimum is not obvious until you hit it. This guide walks through setup from start to finish, including the steps that typically get missed.

## What Google Ads Price Assets Are and How They Work

Price assets attach structured pricing information to your ads in search results. Instead of showing only a headline and description, your ad can display a set of cards, each representing a service, product tier, or package with its price and a direct link to the relevant page on your site. The feature supports both fixed pricing and variable pricing through qualifier options like "from," "up to," or "average," so businesses without set rates can still participate. Any service business with multiple offerings or a tiered pricing structure can use this feature to give searchers more context before they ever reach the landing page.

The types of pricing structures you can represent include individual services, product categories, product tiers (entry, mid, premium), and brand-level categories. If you have a service business with defined package pricing or a product business with a standard tiered catalog, price assets give you a native way to communicate that inside the ad itself without requiring any additional landing page.

## Where to Find the Price Asset Setup in Google Ads

Setting up Google Ads price assets starts at the bottom of the campaign setup interface, which is why most advertisers miss them entirely. Inside your Google Ads account, scroll to the bottom of the setup features section and look for "more asset types." Click that, scroll to "prices," and click "add prices." From there you will see any price assets already created for the account, or an option to create new ones.

This access point sits below the primary setup fields and does not surface prominently during a standard campaign build. We check for underused asset types in [our standard account management review](/blog/google-ads-management-checklist-weekly-system/) because features like price assets cost nothing to activate but add meaningful ad real estate in search results.

## Choosing the Right Price Type and Qualifier

Before building individual price assets, you set two top-level options that apply across the entire pricing structure. The price type describes what your prices represent: services, product tiers, product categories, or brands. For most service businesses, "services" or "product tiers" are the right choice depending on whether you are showing distinct services or a tiered version of one service.

The price qualifier controls how each price is displayed. If pricing is fixed, set no qualifier. If pricing varies by project scope or complexity, the "from," "up to," or "average" qualifiers let you show a realistic range rather than a specific number that may not apply to every situation. Setting "from $250" communicates a starting point without overpromising a specific price to every searcher who might be dealing with a different scope. Keep the language set to English and currency to US Dollars for US campaigns unless you are specifically targeting other markets.

## Building Individual Price Assets (and the Three-Asset Minimum)

Each individual price asset has four required components: a headline, a description, a price amount, and a destination URL. The headline is the name of the service or tier. The description is one short line of supporting detail. The price is the dollar amount (fixed or with your chosen qualifier). The destination URL must link to the specific page on your site that corresponds to that offering, not your homepage.

The character limits on descriptions mean you have to be concise and direct. Use the description to communicate the single most important detail about that service or tier. For example, a tiered service business might have a standard package asset with the package name as the headline, the key included feature as the description, the starting price, and a link to that specific package page.

**The three-asset minimum is the most important thing to know about this feature.** Google Ads requires at least 3 active price assets before the feature shows in search results at all. If you build only two, the assets will be saved in your account but will never display. If your business currently has only two service offerings, you will need to either break one into sub-tiers or add a third qualifying service before price assets can activate.

On the upper end, there is no strict cap. You can go past five price assets if you offer a wide range of services or products, and Google will select the most relevant subset to show in search results.

## Creating Separate Pricing Structures for Different Services

If your business covers genuinely different service categories, you can build separate pricing structures and apply each one at the campaign or ad group level. The setup does not force you to combine everything into one price asset list, which would quickly become cluttered for businesses with distinct service lines.

For example, a business offering both individual consulting sessions and group training workshops could build one price asset set for the sessions (with three or more assets covering different session lengths or formats) and a separate set for the training programs. Each set applies to the campaigns most relevant to that service. After applying one structure and saving, return to the price asset tool and create the second structure for the other service category.

This flexibility matters because pricing information shown in search results should match the specific service a campaign is targeting. A general mix of all your pricing options is less useful to a searcher than a focused set of three to five assets that directly relate to what they searched for. The same logic applies when you are thinking about [conversion tracking setup](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-settings/) and ensuring traffic from each campaign lands on the most relevant page.

## Toggling Price Assets On After You Save

Saving your price assets does not automatically make them active, and this is the step most advertisers miss on the first setup. After saving, navigate to the assets section of the campaign and find your pricing assets in the asset list. Confirm that the status shows as enabled. If the toggle is off, the assets are saved in the account but are not serving in search results.

Toggle the assets on, then click apply. That apply step is the confirmation that activates delivery. Without it, the assets sit dormant. If you set up price assets and they are not showing in search results after a few days, check the toggle status in the asset list before troubleshooting anything else. This is almost always the explanation when assets appear to be set up correctly but are not running.

## How Price Assets Fit Into a Complete Google Ads Account

Price assets are part of a broader strategy of using every available asset type to maximize the information your ads carry and the space they occupy in search results. Across our client accounts, which represent $20M+ in managed ad spend, underused asset types are one of the most consistent gaps we find in [account audits](/blog/google-ads-management-fee-ad-spend-account-audit/). Price assets are especially low-effort to add because they require no budget increase and no campaign restructuring.

Service businesses with defined pricing, particularly those where price awareness helps potential buyers make an informed decision before clicking, tend to benefit most from using them. The underlying logic is direct: if someone sees your pricing range in the ad and still clicks, they have already processed what you charge. That context changes the nature of the interaction when they reach your site.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many price assets do I need to get them to show in Google Ads?**
You need a minimum of 3 active price assets. Google will not display the feature until that threshold is met. If you only have 2 assets saved, they will be stored in your account but will never appear in search results.

**Can I show price ranges instead of fixed prices?**
Yes. The price qualifier options let you show "from," "up to," or "average" pricing. Use these when your pricing varies by project scope, client size, or service complexity. Fixed pricing with no qualifier is also fully supported.

**Do price assets increase my cost-per-click?**
No. Price assets are free to add and do not change your CPC. You pay the same per click regardless of which assets are attached to the ad. The only investment is the time to set up the assets and update them when pricing changes.

**Can different campaigns show different price assets?**
Yes. You can apply separate price asset sets at the campaign or ad group level. Different service lines can show their own pricing structures without combining all your pricing into one list.

**What is the most common mistake when setting up price assets?**
Forgetting to toggle the assets on after saving. The save step stores the assets in the account, but they do not serve until you confirm they are enabled in the asset list and click apply. If price assets are not showing after setup, check this first before troubleshooting anything else.

**Can I use price assets for a business with only two service offerings?**
Not as-is. You need a minimum of three active price assets for the feature to activate. If you only offer two services, consider whether one can be broken into sub-tiers (for example, a standard version and a premium version) to reach the three-asset minimum.
