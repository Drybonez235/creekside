# Who Owns Your Ad Account When the Freelancer Leaves? The Question Every Business Owner Should Ask

Most business owners evaluating a Google Ads freelancer focus on the wrong things. They look at reviews, portfolios, pricing, and turnaround time. Those things are worth checking. But there is one question almost nobody asks, and it is the one that will cost you the most if you skip it.

"When this relationship ends, do I have full ownership and access to everything built for me?"

## The Account Hostage Problem: What Happens When a Freelancer Holds Your Work

Some business owners have been badly burned by agencies and freelancers who hold their work hostage after the engagement ends. This occurs when the freelancer builds your Google Ads campaigns inside their own account rather than inside an account you own. When the relationship ends, you cannot access your campaign history, conversion data, audience lists, or the optimizations built over months of work. You paid for all of it, and you walk away with nothing transferable.

This is not hypothetical. According to Creekside Marketing's direct experience working with business owners and white label agencies: some people have gotten screwed in the past by agencies and freelancers that basically hold their work hostage. The solution is straightforward but must be established before the engagement starts.

At Creekside, clients always have full access to every account built for them. That is not a premium feature. It is the baseline standard.

## How to Verify Account Ownership Before You Hire a Google Ads Freelancer

Before hiring any Google Ads freelancer, confirm account ownership by asking directly: "Will all work be built inside an account I own, and will I have admin-level access at all times?" A trustworthy freelancer answers yes clearly and without qualifications. The question itself reveals whether they have been asked it before and how they handle transparency.

Here is what good answers look like:

- "We build inside your Google Ads account, which you own from day one."
- "You will be listed as account owner throughout the engagement."
- "If we stop working together, you keep everything we built."

Here is what raises concerns:

- "We manage campaigns inside our manager account."
- "Access is provided after the setup phase is complete."
- "Our campaigns belong to our proprietary system."

Asking this question before signing costs nothing. Discovering the answer after months of ad spend has already been committed is where it gets expensive.

## The Generalist Trap: Why Platform Specialization Changes What You Get

Choosing a Google Ads or Meta Ads freelancer who claims expertise across every advertising platform at once carries a specific kind of risk. Based on Creekside Marketing's direct experience across 200+ account audits: if you go with someone who says they are a jack of all trades, typically that means master of none. Google Ads and Meta Ads have different auction mechanics, audience targeting systems, and optimization levers. Managing them well is not the same skill applied in two places.

Creekside structures its operation around a clear division of expertise. Peterson manages Google Ads accounts. His partner Cade is the in-house [Meta Ads](/digital-advertising/meta-ads/) expert. They have worked together for four years. The partnership was built deliberately because each platform requires focused attention, and having a specialist on each one produces different results than splitting one person's attention between both.

When evaluating a freelancer, ask what percentage of their active client work is specifically Google Ads. If the honest answer is less than half, you are looking at someone managing your account alongside a range of other platforms and tools, none of which they have fully mastered.

## Conversion Tracking: The Foundation Most Freelancers Skip Before Recommending Changes

The most consistent finding across Creekside's [Google Ads](/digital-advertising/google-ads/) account audits is broken or incomplete conversion tracking. This matters because smart bidding strategies, the default across most Google Ads accounts today, use conversion data to decide who sees your ads and what Google bids for each click. When that data is wrong, the algorithm optimizes for the wrong thing.

Bad data produces bad results. That is a direct statement from Creekside's Upwork introduction video, and it applies regardless of account size. A $3,000/month account with broken tracking optimizes just as poorly as a $30,000/month account with the same problem. Fixing the tracking first is not optional.

Creekside uses Google Tag Manager for conversion tracking implementation on both Google Ads and Meta Ads accounts. Before any bid strategy is recommended, the tracking foundation is verified: which conversion actions fire, whether tags are working correctly, and whether the actions Google is counting match the outcomes the client actually cares about.

A Google Ads freelancer who wants to change your campaign settings or bid strategy before checking whether your data is accurate has the sequence backwards. Tracking verification comes first, always.

## What Good Account Management Looks Like Across Multiple Clients

One reasonable concern when hiring a freelancer managing multiple clients is whether any individual account gets the attention it needs. The answer depends entirely on the systems behind the management, not on the freelancer's stated capacity.

Creekside manages accounts for white label agencies and business owners directly. Across that volume, each client's account is kept completely separate, with its own documentation, test log, and record of what has been done and why. That record is what allows a second manager to step in if needed, and what makes results from six months ago explainable when they need to be.

A management system that keeps decision history inside the account record rather than inside one person's memory is what makes multi-client management reliable. Ask a potential freelancer what happens if they are unavailable for two weeks. Can someone else pick up the account without starting from scratch? If the answer is no, the entire engagement depends on a single point of failure.

## The Five Questions to Ask Any Google Ads Freelancer Before You Sign

Based on 200+ account audits and $20M+ in ad spend reviewed at Creekside Marketing, these five questions separate well-structured paid ads operations from everyone else. Ask them before any contract is signed. Pay attention to how the freelancer responds, not just what they say.

1. Who will own the Google Ads account when this engagement ends?
2. What percentage of your active client work is specifically Google Ads versus other platforms?
3. Can you walk me through how you audit a new account before making any changes?
4. How do you verify that conversion tracking is accurate before recommending bid strategy changes?
5. If you were unavailable for two weeks, what would another manager need to take over the account?

Freelancers who can answer each of these specifically and clearly operate with real systems. Those who give vague or generalized answers do not.

## FAQ

**Why do some Google Ads agencies restrict client access to their own ad accounts?**

Account access restriction is sometimes used as a retention tactic. When clients cannot see what was built or take their campaign history with them, they are less likely to leave the relationship. This arrangement benefits the agency at the expense of the client. Full admin access from day one is the standard Creekside operates by, and it should be the minimum any client accepts.

**Does it actually matter whether my freelancer specializes in Google Ads or also runs Meta Ads?**

Yes. Google Ads and Meta Ads have meaningfully different auction mechanics, audience targeting systems, conversion tracking requirements, and optimization approaches. Managing both platforms well is not the same skill applied in two places. Creekside separates Google Ads management from Meta Ads management between two specialists precisely because the platforms do not fully overlap, and client results reflect that structure.

**What is the fastest way to check whether a freelancer understands conversion tracking?**

Ask them to audit your existing conversion tracking before proposing any changes. They should be able to tell you exactly what is converting, whether the tags fire correctly, and whether the actions Google is counting match the outcomes your business actually cares about. If they skip this step and jump straight to campaign recommendations, they are starting in the wrong place.

**How do I know my account gets enough attention if the freelancer manages many clients?**

Ask about documentation and systems rather than availability. A well-run operation keeps each account's decision history, test records, and recent changes documented so anyone reviewing the account can follow what happened and why. If that record does not exist, the management depends entirely on one person's memory, which is a fragile foundation for your ad spend.

**Is it a red flag if a Google Ads freelancer uses a manager account?**

Not by itself. Manager accounts are a standard part of how agencies handle multiple clients. The question is whether YOUR account exists as a standalone account you own, housed under their manager account for administrative convenience, or whether campaigns are built directly inside their manager account. The distinction determines what you keep if the relationship ends.
