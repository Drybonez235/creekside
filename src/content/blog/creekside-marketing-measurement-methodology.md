## How We Define and Track Conversions

A conversion in Creekside's reporting is a platform-tracked action that has been verified against a secondary data source. We do not count page views, scroll depth, or time-on-site as conversions. Here is what qualifies:

**Primary conversion actions (what we optimize toward):**
- Phone calls lasting 60+ seconds (tracked via call tracking with recording)
- Form submissions that include valid contact information
- E-commerce purchases with confirmed transaction data
- Booked appointments confirmed in the client's scheduling system

**Verification layer:**
Every conversion goes through a verification step before it enters our performance reporting. For lead generation accounts, we cross-reference ad platform conversion data against the client's CRM, call tracking platform, or intake system. For e-commerce, we reconcile Google Ads reported revenue against Shopify, WooCommerce, or the client's order management system.

This matters because ad platforms over-count. Google Ads' default conversion settings include repeat conversions, view-through conversions, and modeled conversions. We configure accounts to count only the actions that represent real business outcomes, and we verify the count independently.

**Conversion tracking audit (every new account):**
Before we report any performance data, every account goes through a conversion tracking verification:
- Confirm the correct conversion actions are set as primary
- Verify tag firing matches actual form submissions and calls
- Check for duplicate conversion counting
- Confirm attribution window settings match the client's sales cycle
- Validate that Google Ads reported conversions align within 10% of CRM data

Accounts with broken or inflated conversion tracking get fixed before any optimization begins. Optimizing toward bad data produces bad results. We have documented cases where fixing conversion tracking alone changed account performance dramatically. One account (GLP-1 weight loss brand) had duplicate conversion tracking and five other structural issues. After the fix, the account showed 9.74x ROAS on $46.8K in 30-day spend, but those results were invisible before the tracking was corrected.

## How We Calculate ROAS

ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) in Creekside's reporting means attributed revenue divided by ad spend. Not estimated revenue. Not projected revenue. Not "potential" revenue based on average deal size.

**For e-commerce clients:**
ROAS = total attributed purchase revenue from the ad platform / total ad spend for the same period. We reconcile this against the store's actual order data monthly to ensure the ad platform is not over-reporting.

**For lead generation clients:**
We calculate ROAS using the client's actual revenue data when available. When exact revenue is not available (common in services businesses that close deals offline), we use the client's confirmed average deal value multiplied by their confirmed close rate, multiplied by verified lead count. We document which method is used for each client.

**What we exclude from ROAS calculations:**
- Branded search conversions are reported separately because they inflate ROAS. A customer searching your business name was likely coming to you anyway.
- View-through conversions are excluded from primary ROAS reporting. They appear in supplemental reporting only.
- We do not include assisted conversions in primary ROAS figures.

When we publish a ROAS number (like the 9.74x for the GLP-1 brand or the 6.1x for Chagrin Valley Beauty), the calculation method is documented in the corresponding case study.

## How We Measure Cost Per Lead

Cost per lead in Creekside's reporting is total ad spend divided by the number of verified leads. The "verified" part is critical because raw Google Ads conversion counts include spam form submissions, accidental clicks, and duplicate entries.

**Our filtering process:**
1. Pull total conversions from the ad platform for the reporting period
2. Cross-reference against the client's CRM or intake system
3. Remove spam submissions (form bots, test entries, competitors)
4. Remove duplicate leads (same person submitting twice)
5. Report CPL based on the cleaned lead count

This is why our reported CPL figures tend to be higher than raw platform numbers but more accurate than what agencies typically report. A "$30 cost per lead" that includes 40% spam is actually a $50 cost per lead. We report the $50.

## Our 85-Criteria Account Audit Standard

Every new client account goes through an 85-criteria audit before we make any changes. The audit covers five categories:

**1. Conversion Tracking (18 criteria)**
Tag implementation, firing verification, attribution settings, duplicate detection, cross-domain tracking, phone call tracking configuration, offline conversion import setup.

**2. Account Structure (20 criteria)**
Campaign organization, ad group granularity, keyword-to-ad-group alignment, match type distribution, negative keyword coverage, shared budget conflicts, campaign type appropriateness.

**3. Targeting (15 criteria)**
Geographic targeting accuracy (presence vs. interest), audience segment configuration (observation vs. targeting), device bid adjustments, ad schedule alignment with business hours, language settings.

**4. Bidding (17 criteria)**
Strategy selection appropriateness for data volume, target CPA/ROAS calibration, portfolio bid strategy conflicts, budget-to-bid alignment, conversion action selection for bidding.

**5. Creative & Extensions (15 criteria)**
Ad copy relevance scoring, RSA pin strategy, extension/asset coverage (sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets, call, location), landing page alignment, mobile experience.

**How we use audit results:**
The audit produces a scored report that identifies the highest-impact fixes. We prioritize structural issues (broken tracking, wrong targeting settings) over optimization issues (bid adjustments, ad copy testing) because structural problems make optimization data unreliable.

Our published case studies reference audit findings when relevant. For example, the Florida Awnings account audit found conversion tracking pulling from the wrong US regions, meaning every optimization decision was based on geographically incorrect data.

## FAQ

### Why should I trust agency-reported data over industry surveys?

Industry surveys (WordStream, HubSpot, etc.) aggregate self-reported data across all skill levels, including poorly managed accounts with broken tracking. Our data comes from professionally managed accounts where conversion tracking has been audited and verified. The data is more reliable because the measurement methodology is controlled.

### Do you share raw campaign data?

We share anonymized performance metrics in case studies with client permission. Raw dashboard access is available to active clients for their own accounts. We do not publish raw data for confidentiality reasons, but every published metric can be traced to its source campaign.

### How do your benchmarks account for geographic differences?

Our benchmarks represent ranges specifically because geography moves costs significantly. A $15 CPC in Nashville and a $45 CPC in Los Angeles for the same keyword are both within the stated range. The industry-level ranges on our benchmark page capture this geographic variation.

### What happens when your tracking disagrees with the client's data?

We investigate the discrepancy. Common causes include attribution window differences, CRM deduplication rules, or offline conversions not being imported. We reconcile until both systems agree within 10%, then document the reconciliation method for ongoing reporting.
