Here is something we say at Creekside Marketing that catches business owners off guard: "You already know everything about your business. We just need to tell you how to put it into ads so you can make more money."

That comes directly from a recent conversation Peterson Rainey had about how Creekside actually operates. And it runs against the way most business owners evaluate google ads agency industry experience when shopping for help.

They quiz agencies on industry background. They want to know if you have run dental campaigns, contractor campaigns, law firm campaigns. The implicit test is: does this person understand our world?

That is the wrong test. Here is what to ask instead.

## Does Your Google Ads Agency Need Industry Experience to Get Results?

Short answer: no. A Google Ads agency does not need deep industry experience to run campaigns that produce strong results. They need deep Google Ads experience. That distinction sounds minor, but it reframes every conversation you have when evaluating an agency. You are not looking for someone who understands your industry. You are looking for someone who understands bid strategy, conversion tracking, and campaign architecture well enough to translate what you know into ads that make money.

Peterson said it plainly: "I don't need to learn everything about your business. You already know everything about your business. I just need to tell you how to put it into ads so you can make more money."

The business knowledge lives with you. The ads expertise lives with the agency. When those two things stay in their lanes, the relationship works. When an agency spends your first three months getting an education on your industry, you are paying for their learning curve, not your results.

## What a Google Ads Agency Actually Brings Beyond Industry Background

A competent agency brings platform expertise most business owners have spent years not building. They understand how Google's auction works, how Quality Score affects your actual cost-per-click, how to structure bid strategy transitions without losing the algorithmic data you have already accumulated, and how to read search term reports to catch the negative keyword gaps draining budget quietly every week.

None of that requires industry knowledge. It requires knowing the platform cold.

What you bring is the content. You know what questions customers ask before buying, what objections surface in sales calls, what makes someone choose you over the competitor down the street, and what your margins actually support in terms of acceptable cost per lead. A tight agency intake process should extract all of that in a single session.

After that, the agency executes. You should not be spending four months educating your ads manager on how your industry works. If that is happening, you are paying a tuition bill you never signed up for.

## The Real Difference Between Managing Ads and Growing a Business

Look, most agencies pitch themselves as "growth partners" and then deliver monthly reports full of platform metrics. There is a real version of that distinction, and understanding it changes how you structure the relationship from the start. An agency that is genuinely focused on growth measures different things than one that is focused on managing a media budget.

At Creekside, the stated goal is not higher click-through rates or more impressions. Peterson described what Creekside actually wants from client relationships in a recent conversation: "We both like growing businesses. It doesn't really matter the business. We would work just as hard to build out the systems." And separately: "I genuinely like helping people that already know they're good at what they do. They need help with the business side of things."

That framing changes what you measure. Cost per acquisition over time, lead quality trends, and revenue tied back to spend are the metrics that tell you whether the relationship is working. Any agency that leads their reports with impressions and reach numbers without connecting them to acquisition costs is telling you something about how they define their job.

Platform metrics are not the goal. Business growth is the goal. Ads are the mechanism.

## What to Evaluate When Hiring a Google Ads Agency

If google ads agency industry experience is not the primary filter, use these instead. They actually predict whether the relationship will produce results. Run through them in every first conversation:

**Can they explain their bid strategy process from launch through optimization?** The move from Maximize Clicks to Maximize Conversions to Target CPA is one of the highest-impact decisions in any account. An agency that cannot tell you what data triggers each transition, or that jumps straight to Target CPA on a brand-new account with zero conversion history, does not have a real process. For a detailed look at how that progression should work, read [the 90-day Google Ads plan with target metrics](/blog/google-ads-90-day-plan-target-metrics-goals/).

**Do they ask about your goals before talking about the platform?** Agencies that open with campaign type options and ad formats before understanding what you are trying to accomplish are building toward the wrong goals. Business context comes before platform configuration every time.

**Will they separate clearly what they own from what you own?** You own the business, the offer, the post-click experience, and the customer relationship. They own campaign architecture, bids, ad copy, and optimization decisions. Clean ownership on both sides means clean accountability when things go wrong.

**Can they show you what they have done for similar businesses?** Peterson described the version of Creekside that actually works: "people begging to work with us because they see all the stuff that we've been able to do for all these other businesses." That track record, shown clearly with real outcomes, is the credibility signal that matters. ## FAQ

**Do I need a Google Ads agency that specializes in my industry?**

Specialization can shorten the intake process. But a rigorous general process, a clear track record of improving cost per acquisition, and strong accountability will outperform industry specialization most of the time. Ask for specific results from accounts similar to yours before assuming a "specialist" label means better work. Lots of specialists run cookie-cutter setups inside their niche.

**How long should onboarding take before campaigns go live?**

A defined process should have campaigns live within two to three weeks of signing. Extended onboarding beyond that usually means the agency does not have a repeatable launch process. Some accounts genuinely need more setup time, particularly if conversion tracking is broken or there are major account structure problems to fix. But the "we need to learn your industry" phase should not be the bottleneck.

**What is the biggest mistake business owners make when working with an agency?**

Measuring the agency on the wrong things. Most default to checking impressions, reach, and CTR because those numbers are visible and easy to find in a report. The metrics that actually matter are cost per acquisition, lead quality trend over time, and revenue impact. An agency that consistently leads with reach numbers and avoids talking about acquisition costs is signaling what they think they are accountable for.

**What should a good 90-day outcome look like?**

By day 90, you should have a reliable cost per acquisition, enough conversion volume to make confident bid strategy decisions, and a clear plan for the next quarter. If you are still in "data collection mode" at day 90, something about the account setup needs to be addressed before you proceed. That is not a normal timeline, it is a problem. For a breakdown of what the milestone targets should look like, read [the full 90-day Google Ads plan](/blog/google-ads-90-day-plan-target-metrics-goals/).

**When does industry experience actually matter?**

It matters most for writing ad copy and understanding the objections that show up in search queries. A dental patient searching for veneers types different things than a homeowner searching for roof repair. But a good agency learns this in your intake process, not from years of running the same playbook for the same vertical. The copy gets better fast. The platform mechanics are the same across every industry.
