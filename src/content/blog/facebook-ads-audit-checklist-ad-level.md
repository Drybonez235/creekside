## Why Most Facebook Ads Audits Stop Too Early

Running a Facebook ads audit checklist at the campaign and ad set levels is a start, but most accounts bleed budget from errors at the ad level that those higher-level reviews never catch. A missing headline, a blank carousel card, or a wrong destination can quietly drain spend while everything above it looks fine. According to Creekside Marketing's review of new accounts at onboarding, ad-level gaps show up in the majority of first audits.

This post is based on a video Peterson published on the Creekside Marketing YouTube channel: [Facebook Ads Audit | Ads Level](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eK9U1sCvBe4). It covers the ad-level tier of a three-part audit series. The campaign and ad set levels each have their own video. This tier is where the most fixable, highest-impact errors sit. You can have a technically correct campaign structure and still waste money because of what is happening at the ad level.

## The Complete Facebook Ads Audit Checklist: 15 Ad-Level Points

Here are all 15 checkpoints in order. Work through them before every campaign launch and after any major creative refresh. Each should be a clear yes before you publish. If you hit a no, fix it first. These are the same points we check on every new account Creekside takes on and the same ones we audit each quarter on existing accounts.

**1. Do you have all the ads you need?**

Sounds obvious. It gets missed. If you are running a wellness campaign for a med spa and you want a general wellness ad plus a body sculpting offer, you need two separate ads. Not one ad trying to carry both. Combining them splits your message and confuses how the budget allocates between objectives.

**2. Is each ad named specifically?**

Name each ad based on what it does. In the audit walkthrough, "copy" and "biot" are used to distinguish a standard creative from a body sculpting ad. When you are managing accounts with 10 or more active ads, you should be able to identify any one of them from its name alone, without opening it.

**3. Are you connected to the right Facebook and Instagram page?**

This is one of the most common ad-level errors in agency work. When you run ads for a client inside your own Ads Manager, the platform defaults to the page connected to the account login. That is YOUR page, not the client's. You have to manually switch it in the identity section for every ad you create.

According to Creekside Marketing's experience managing multiple client accounts in a single Ads Manager, this mistake happens across skill levels. Miss it and all social proof (likes, comments, shares) from that ad accumulates on your page instead of the client's. It also affects retargeting audience data.

**4. Is "Create an Ad" selected instead of "Use Existing Post"?**

Under Ad Setup, choose "Create an Ad" in almost all cases. The exception is when you have an organic post with significant existing engagement you want to extend. Otherwise, create a new creative. Using an existing post costs you format flexibility and limits your CTA options. The control you get from a new ad is worth more than recycled engagement.

**5. Are you running carousel format?**

For most campaigns, carousel is the right choice. It shows users multiple images or videos within a single ad unit, keeps them interacting longer, and gives Meta more engagement signal to work with when finding your audience. A single image gets one moment to make an impression. A carousel gets three to five.

Look, there are campaigns where single image makes sense. But if you cannot name a specific reason, run carousel.

**6. Does every carousel card have an image selected?**

When you first create a carousel, Meta adds a blank placeholder card. You have to manually assign an image or video to it. A card without content runs as a gray block or blank space. It looks incomplete and signals to Meta that the creative is not ready.

Check every card individually. Do not assume the platform will flag it before you publish.

**7. Does every card have a headline written?**

A blank headline is a wasted opportunity. Every card has that field. Fill it. More information in the ad means more context for the person deciding whether to click, and more material for Meta to optimize with. There is no upside to leaving it empty.

**8. Does every card have a description written?**

Same principle as headlines. Descriptions show in many Meta placements. Fill every field. You are already paying for the placement. Maximize the copy space you have.

**9. Do the descriptions apply to all cards, or are they card-specific?**

Here is where judgment matters. Meta gives you the option to apply the same description to every card. Whether to use it depends on the campaign.

If every card promotes the same offer, say "get your first lawn mowed for $25," apply it to all cards. If one card is a general service ad and another features a limited-time promotion, those cards need different copy. A 25% off offer on one card should not have the same description as a standard brand card.

The key is being intentional. Do not let this default without thinking through it.

**10. Are you maximizing primary text variations?**

Meta allows up to five primary text variations per ad. Use all five. They need to be genuinely different from each other, not small rewrites of the same line. One variation can lead with the price point. Another leads with the outcome. Another opens with the problem. Give Meta real material to test against different audience segments.

An ad with two nearly identical primary texts is leaving optimization capacity on the table.

**11. Does every primary text include a call to action?**

This is separate from the CTA button. Inside each primary text variation, include a direct CTA: a phone number, "book now," a link to the form, "call us today." Do not make people guess what to do next. Tell them. The CTA button handles one touchpoint. The primary text handles another.

**12. Is the correct CTA button selected?**

Match the button to the campaign goal. Lead gen with an instant form gets "Get Offer" or "Sign Up." A call campaign gets "Call Now." Traffic to a website gets a click-oriented option. The button and the destination should reinforce the same action. When they point in different directions, it creates friction right at the point of conversion.

**13. Are all ad enhancements enabled?**

Under the enhancements section, three options should be on:

- **Highlighted Carousel Card**: Adds your business page profile to the last card in the carousel. Free brand visibility.
- **Music**: Meta can add background audio to the ad. It grabs attention from users scrolling with sound on.
- **Dynamic Descriptions**: Meta automatically surfaces whichever description is performing best for each user. This is the mechanical reason you need multiple descriptions. Without them, there is nothing to optimize.

There is no reason to disable any of these. Leave them all on.

**14. Is the correct destination selected?**

Check that the destination matches the campaign objective. A lead gen campaign using an instant form should point to the form. A traffic campaign should point to the correct website URL. A mismatch here means a technically live ad driving people to the wrong place. It happens more often than it should, and the damage is invisible until you look at where your traffic is actually landing.

**15. Has tracking been applied?**

The minimum is offline events. The ideal is website pixel events plus offline events together. If you are running a call campaign with no website destination, offline tracking is how you measure how many calls the campaign drives. Without it, you are spending with no feedback loop and no basis for optimization.

If you have the Meta pixel installed and your campaign drives to a website, use it. Pixel data feeds into retargeting audiences, lookalike audiences, and conversion optimization. It makes the whole account better over time, not just the individual campaign.

## The Setup Error That Shows Up Most

The page connection error (checkpoint 3) is the one that catches agencies off guard most consistently. It is not an obvious mistake. The platform does not warn you. It simply defaults to your own page, and if you do not actively check, the ad publishes under your account identity instead of the client's.

Miss it once and you have run ads building social proof and audience data for the wrong page. That data does not transfer. The client's page does not benefit from it. And if the same mistake runs for multiple campaigns before anyone catches it, you have a real problem to explain.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Facebook Ads Audits

**How long does an ad-level audit take?**

Working through all 15 checkpoints takes roughly 10 to 15 minutes per ad. A campaign with four ads is about an hour of focused review. Do it before every launch. Do not skip it because the campaign "looks ready."

**Do I need all five primary text variations?**

You can publish with fewer. You should not. Each variation is material Meta can use to find the best message for different audience segments. Using two or three variations means less optimization data and slower performance improvement.

**When does using an existing post instead of a new ad actually make sense?**

When that post already has significant organic reach and engagement you want to extend into paid. Otherwise, create a new ad. The format control and CTA flexibility you get with a new creative are worth more than borrowed engagement from an old post.

**What if we do not have the Meta pixel installed?**

Install it before spending money on any campaign driving to a website. Offline events are a fallback for call-only campaigns, but any traffic campaign needs the pixel. Without it, you cannot build retargeting audiences, track website conversions accurately, or feed conversion data back into Meta's algorithm.

**Why does carousel consistently outperform single image for service businesses?**

Carousel keeps users interacting longer. More interaction means more signal for Meta about who responds to your ads and what messaging connects. Single image gets one frame to make an impression. Carousel gets multiple. For most service businesses running lead gen, that extra engagement is what separates a campaign that scales from one that stalls.
