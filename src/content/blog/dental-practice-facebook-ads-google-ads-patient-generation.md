## Why Facebook Ads and Google Ads Work Differently for Dental Practices

Meta and Google target opposite moments in the patient journey, and a patient who sees you on both platforms is far more likely to book than one who only sees you on one. That's the core of why this system works, and why most single-platform dental ad campaigns underperform. Most dentists running only Google are capping their volume at whatever search demand already exists in their market. Most dentists running only Meta are generating interest with no capture mechanism when that interest turns into a search. The practices filling chairs consistently are running both, with each platform doing its specific job.

The patient journey looks like this: someone scrolls Instagram and sees a veneer transformation. They didn't know they wanted veneers before. Now they do. They don't book from Instagram. They close the app, open Google that afternoon or the next morning, and type "porcelain veneers cost" or "no prep veneers near me." If you're on Google when they search, you get the booking. If you're not, the practice that appears first does.

Meta creates the interest. Google catches it when that interest becomes a search. Both moments require their own setup.

## How We Build the Facebook Ads System for Cosmetic Dentistry

Don't boost posts. Boosting locks you out of the targeting controls and creative testing tools that determine whether you pay $15 per lead or $75 per lead on cosmetic dental campaigns. Ads Manager gives you placement selection, language targeting, age and gender segmentation, and the ability to run true A/B tests inside a single ad set. Boosting skips all of that and hands the algorithm a much weaker signal about who you want to reach.

For the cosmetic dental practice in our case study, the campaign objective is engagement, targeting Instagram DMs. That's a deliberate choice. When someone messages a practice directly about veneers, they're warmer than a cold form fill on a site they've never visited. The DM is the starting point for a real conversation.

At the ad set level, specificity wins:

- Placement: Instagram only (not the full Meta network)
- Geography: a defined radius around the practice, not the full metro
- Language: English. This sounds obvious. It gets skipped constantly, and ads end up shown to the wrong audience.
- Age and gender: matched to the cosmetic dental buyer, not left broad

Inside each ad set, we run multiple ads. Meta's Andromeda update lets the algorithm pit them against each other and surface a winner. We let it do that work.

**The three creative types that work consistently for cosmetic dental:**

| Creative Type | Why It Stops the Scroll |
|, , , , |, , , , , , |
| Patient testimonials | Real reactions to real results; builds trust fast |
| Before-and-after carousels | Visual, swipeable, high scroll-stop rate |
| Founder value proposition video | Explains why this practice, why now |

Creatives are the biggest variable on Meta. Get them wrong and no amount of targeting precision saves the account. For this practice, approximately $96,000 in spend over one month generated roughly 6,200 leads at about $15 each. The average smile makeover case is worth around $1,100. That cost efficiency comes from creatives that stop people while they scroll, not from clever bid settings.

## How We Build the Google Ads System for a Cosmetic Dental Practice

Consistent results week after week, not one good month, is the signal that a Google Ads account is built correctly. From January through early July of this year, the Google Ads account for this practice generated 937 qualified leads at $102 each on $95,000 in total spend. Volume held steady. Cost per conversion held steady. No months where the account fell off a cliff. That consistency is structural, not luck.

We run three campaign types, and each one has a specific job:

| Campaign Type | Job |
|, , , -|, -|
| Search | Captures patients actively searching "veneers near me," "porcelain veneers cost," "no prep veneers [city]" |
| Performance Max | Finds new search terms and audiences across Google's network |
| Demand Gen | Retargets people who visited the site but didn't call or submit a form |

**Ad group structure matters more than most dentists realize.** Inside the search campaign, we run two separate ad groups: one for no-prep veneers, one for porcelain veneers. Same procedure, different buyers. The no-prep patient cares about minimal invasion and no drilling. The porcelain patient cares about quality and longevity. One ad group can't speak to both audiences effectively. Splitting them lets us tailor every headline to what each group actually cares about.

Brand keywords go in their own campaign, isolated from service campaigns. Two reasons: branded searches convert at a higher rate, and keeping them separate protects that data from inflating service campaign metrics. If a competitor is bidding on the practice name, the brand campaign catches it.

**The match type progression is where most Google Ads accounts go wrong from the start.** When we launch a new campaign, we stay tight: phrase match and exact match only. We control which searches trigger ads while Google builds a data set on what a qualified lead looks like. Once we hit around 10 quality conversions, we open to broad match. At that point, Google knows who the ideal patient is. Broad match then finds search variations we'd never have thought to include ourselves. Start with broad match and zero conversion data, and you're telling Google to spend the budget with no guidance. That's how you burn through $5,000 in two weeks with nothing to show.

The same threshold logic applies to bidding. New campaigns start on maximize conversions, no target set, just volume. Once the account has around 30 conversions and cost has stabilized, we switch to target CPA set slightly below the current cost. That forces Google to get more efficient rather than just maintain the status quo. Both transitions need to be earned with data. You can't shortcut them.

**The 6,000+ negative keyword list.** Most advertisers don't treat the negative keyword list as a strategic asset. We block over 6,000 searches in this account: dental insurance, teeth cleaning, dentures, dental schools, and every variation of them. Not a single dollar is spent on someone looking for something the practice doesn't offer. The list grows every week because we review actual search terms weekly, and anything that doesn't belong gets added as a negative immediately. One focused week of search term review can reallocate hundreds of dollars to better traffic.

**Conversion tracking is the foundation.** We track only two things: phone calls over 60 seconds and qualified form fills where the patient passes screening questions and can actually be reached. A 5-second hang-up is not a lead. A form fill with no valid contact is not a lead. Tracking these as conversions tells Google to find more of the wrong people. Tighten the definition, and the account starts finding better traffic.

One asset that directly reduces cost per booking: financing sitelinks. Cost is the most common objection to veneers. When someone searching for "porcelain veneers cost" sees "2-year 0% financing" in the ad before they click, that objection is handled. Call assets run on a schedule that matches business hours only. There's no reason to pay for a phone call to an empty front desk.

## The Third Element: Lead Response and Closed-Loop Attribution

Getting the lead is half the battle. The half most practices underestimate happens in the next 5 minutes.

Every lead from both platforms flows into the CRM and moves through stages: contact added, qualified hot lead, booked appointment, sold. If someone doesn't pick up when we call, they don't disappear. They drop into a follow-up sequence. Nothing goes unworked. Look, the 5-minute response window is not a best practice recommendation. It's the difference between a booked appointment and a patient who already called the practice down the street.

Here's the part that closes the loop on the whole system: when a lead converts and gets tagged as a qualified patient in the CRM, that data goes back to Meta and Google. The platforms learn exactly what a qualified patient looks like. The accounts that have been running this system for 6, 12, 18 months get more efficient over time because the closed-loop data improves targeting every single week. That compounding effect is what separates a mature account from one that resets each month.

Most practices think they're running a Meta campaign and a Google campaign. The ones getting the best results are running one system with three components: demand generation, demand capture, and lead conversion. Each one feeds the next.

## FAQ

**What Facebook ad objective works best for a cosmetic dental practice?**
Engagement targeting Instagram DMs consistently outperforms lead form and traffic objectives for high-value cosmetic services. The DM conversation starts warmer than a cold form fill on an unfamiliar site, and the booking rate reflects that difference.

**How many conversions do you need before switching Google Ads to broad match?**
Around 10 quality conversions. Before that, the algorithm doesn't have enough data to understand what a qualified patient looks like. Opening to broad match before that threshold means spending budget on low-quality traffic that generates misleading conversion data.

**When should a dental practice switch Google Ads bidding from maximize conversions to target CPA?**
Around 30 conversions with a stable cost per conversion. The switch should happen when you have enough data that setting a target CPA gives Google something real to optimize toward. Setting it too early means the algorithm overreacts to statistical noise and drives cost up instead of down.

**Why run Demand Gen separately from Performance Max?**
PMax can only target lookalike audiences. It cannot show an ad to the specific person who visited your website yesterday and didn't call. Demand Gen handles true retargeting: getting back in front of website visitors, past engagers, and people who clicked but didn't convert. Different job, different campaign.

**Does the lead response time really affect dental bookings that much?**
Yes. Respond within 5 minutes and your booking rate holds. Wait longer and it drops significantly. By the time a practice batch-processes their leads at the end of the day, many of those patients have already booked somewhere else.
