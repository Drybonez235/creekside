## Why the Google Ads Account Ownership Problem Is More Common Than You Think

The Google Ads account ownership question, who actually controls your campaign history and data, is something we encounter constantly. After auditing more than 200 Google Ads and Meta Ads accounts representing more than $20 million in ad spend, we have seen what happens to businesses that let an agency control access to their own data: they lose years of campaign history, audience lists, and conversion data the moment the relationship ends.

We ask for account access at the start of every engagement. How a business responds to that request tells us a great deal about what kind of agency they worked with before.

This post is based on a video Peterson published on the Creekside Marketing YouTube channel: [Upwork Profile Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bg7fo2VudZk).

## Why the First Thing We Do Is an Audit

When a business comes to us already running Google Ads or Meta Ads, we start with a structured audit before changing a single campaign setting. The reason is straightforward: you cannot fix what you cannot diagnose, and a change made without understanding the current state of the account often creates more problems than it solves.

Our audit process covers four areas: structural errors inside campaign setups, wasted spend caused by objectives misaligned with actual business goals, tracking discrepancies that corrupt how Google's algorithm learns, and platform-specific mistakes that stack quietly over months without triggering any obvious alert. This framework was built by auditing dozens of failing accounts across different industries and budgets, not taken from a certification course.

The audit also tells us something else: who actually built this account and whether they understood what they were doing. You can read the history of an account from its architecture. A specialist builds it differently than a generalist. Someone who understands conversion tracking builds it differently than someone who pressed "Start Campaign" and moved on.

## The Google Ads Account Ownership Problem: When Agencies Hold Your Data Hostage

When a business owner lets an agency control their Google Ads account rather than granting manager access to an account the business owns, the agency holds leverage over your campaign history, your audience lists, and your conversion data. When the relationship ends, getting access back can be difficult or impossible. We have encountered this enough times that we built a non-negotiable rule into every engagement: every account we build belongs to the client, and you always have full access.

Some businesses come to us after an agency relationship ended badly. The agency built the Google Ads account under their own MCC, ran the campaigns, and when the contract ended, either refused to transfer access or made the process slow enough that the business simply walked away. Years of campaign data, audience lists, conversion history: gone.

We found that some people have gotten burned by agencies and freelancers who hold their work hostage. So you will not deal with that working with us. We believe that if we are doing good work, we do not need to hide it. According to Creekside Marketing's experience across 200+ account audits: the businesses most vulnerable to this problem hired their first paid ads agency without asking the account ownership question upfront. It seems like a minor detail until the relationship ends.

## What Transparent Reporting Actually Means in Practice

Telling clients they have access is not the same as building a reporting system that gives them genuine visibility into what is happening in their account. Most agencies produce reports. Fewer actually show clients how to read the account itself, and the difference matters when something goes wrong and you need to know why.

On the Meta Ads side, my partner Cade maintains a daily management checklist that he works through every day, sometimes twice per day, making notes that create a paper trail for each account. At any point, a client can ask what changed in their Meta account on a specific date and there is a documented answer. Not an estimate. An actual record.

On the Google Ads side, we use a management system that keeps each client account completely separate and tracks exactly what has been done and when. When you are managing campaigns across multiple clients simultaneously, that organizational discipline is what prevents problems from slipping through.

Based on Creekside Marketing's management approach: the paper trail is not just for the client. It is how we maintain quality across a portfolio without things falling through the cracks.

The analytical background is not a credential. It is a method. I am a mechanical engineer by training. My partner Cade holds an MBA with an accounting focus. Neither of us came up through traditional marketing. That background shapes how we actually read a paid ads account, and it is genuinely uncommon in this industry.

Paid advertising is fundamentally a data problem. Every campaign decision is based on signals: which keywords are converting, which audiences are profitable, which creative is outperforming the control. A mechanical engineer approaches a Google Ads account the way they approach any system: find the inputs, trace them to the outputs, identify where the system is leaking value. An accountant looks at budget allocation the way they look at a balance sheet: every number connects to something else, and nothing is happening in isolation.

We work as specialists, not generalists. I focus on [Google Ads](/digital-advertising/google-ads/). Cade focuses on [Meta Ads](/digital-advertising/meta-ads/). We have been working together for four years. We have audited enough accounts built by generalists to believe that strongly.

## Conversion Tracking: The Foundation That Agencies Skip

If one thing consistently separates accounts that perform from accounts that do not, it is conversion tracking. Bad data in equals bad results out. This is not advisory; it is the literal mechanism by which Google's machine learning operates. When you tell Google Smart Bidding to optimize for conversions, Google looks at every signal in your account and bids more aggressively toward users who look like your converters. If those conversion signals are wrong, Google learns from corrupted data and CPAs climb for no obvious reason.

We place heavy emphasis on getting conversion tracking right before optimizing anything else. We use Google Tag Manager for this work, both for Google Ads and Meta Ads setups. GTM-based tracking is more reliable than direct pixel implementations because it does not depend on a single tag firing correctly on every page load.

Based on Creekside Marketing's audit experience: tracking problems are among the most common findings in underperforming accounts, and they are almost always invisible to the business owner. The account reports conversions. The conversions look plausible. But the actual count is either inflated by duplicate tag firing or deflated by missed events. Google optimizes toward the wrong signal.

This connects directly to the account ownership question. If you cannot get into your own account, you cannot audit your own conversion tracking. You are trusting data you cannot inspect.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does it mean for a Google Ads agency to hold your account hostage?**

It means the agency built or manages your Google Ads campaigns under their own Google Ads Manager account (MCC) rather than granting manager access to an account you own. When the relationship ends, they control whether you can access your campaign history, audience lists, and conversion data. The right structure is always for the business to own the account and the agency to operate as a manager.

**How do I check if I actually own my Google Ads account?**

Log into ads.google.com. If you can see the account and its full campaign history, you own it or have full access. Check the account settings to see who has manager access listed. If the primary owner is an email address you do not recognize, ask the agency to initiate an access transfer while the relationship is still active.

**How do I verify that my conversion tracking is accurate?**

Inside Google Ads, go to Tools and then Conversions. Review each conversion action: confirm the category matches what you are actually tracking, that the current status shows Recording conversions, and that the count method matches your business model. If a conversion action shows zero conversions in the last 30 days and you know leads are coming in, that is a tracking problem. Google Tag Manager's Preview mode lets you verify that tags are actually firing on the events you care about.

**Why does the specialist vs. generalist distinction affect my results?**

Google Ads and Meta Ads have fundamentally different auction mechanics, optimization signals, and creative requirements. Someone who works in one platform all day builds pattern recognition that a generalist cannot replicate. According to Creekside Marketing's experience across 200+ accounts: specialists catch structural issues faster and adapt to platform changes more quickly because they are seeing those changes across their entire portfolio simultaneously.

**What should the first 90 days with a paid ads agency include?**

A real 90-day plan is specific: which campaigns are being built or restructured and when, what the bidding strategy starts at and when it changes, what conversion threshold triggers a strategy shift, and what communication you can expect at each stage. It should account for Google's learning period. A plan that promises results in the first two weeks without acknowledging how long Smart Bidding takes to learn is not grounded in how the platform actually works.
