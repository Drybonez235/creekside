# Before You Run a Single Ad: The 5-Component Digital Marketing Strategy Framework We Use for Every Client

Most businesses don't fail at digital marketing because they can't run ads. They fail because they started running a comprehensive digital marketing strategy without actually having one. Google Ads by itself is not a strategy. Facebook Ads by itself is not a strategy. Running both without SEO, without a funnel, and without a retention layer is just spend.

At Creekside Marketing, we've spent years managing $20M+ in ad spend across Google Ads and Meta Ads. This post walks through the full strategy framework we use for every client, based on a process Peterson demonstrated on the [Creekside Marketing YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IXm4lsV0mlA). The framework covers all 5 components of a complete strategy, starts from nothing but a business website, and takes under 5 minutes to build.

## Why Most Businesses Start in the Wrong Place

Most businesses start with channels. "Let's run Google Ads." "Let's do Facebook." "We need SEO." Each gets its own budget, its own timeline, its own expectations. What you end up with is a collection of tactics with no connective tissue, no funnel logic, and no retention plan. A complete digital marketing strategy starts with the business, not the platforms.

The two inputs that matter most are what the business offers and what differentiates it from competitors. Everything else follows from those. According to Creekside Marketing's demonstrated workflow, you can get both from a single source: the business's own website. The services page tells you what they offer. The about page tells you what sets them apart. You don't need weeks of discovery calls to start building a real strategy.

That insight has practical weight. It means agencies can build a complete strategy before a prospect has even signed. It means business owners can stress-test their own positioning without paying a consultant. It means the excuse of "I don't have enough information yet" almost never holds.

## The 4 Inputs the Framework Starts With

The framework Creekside Marketing uses begins with four inputs. That's it. Four pieces of information, and you have enough to build a complete strategy across all five components.

**1. Business name and type.** The starting point. For the example demonstrated in our workflow, we used a full-service auto repair shop we manage [Google Ads](/digital-advertising/google-ads/) for in the Atlanta area.

**2. Service description.** Pulled directly from the business website. For the auto shop, that includes the specific types of vehicles they service and the range of repairs they handle. You copy from their services page and paste. No internal knowledge required.

**3. Key differentiators.** These go in one at a time. For the auto shop: ASE-certified mechanics. Family-owned and operated. Specific service capabilities that separate them from the national chains. These are the points that give the strategy its specific competitive positioning.

**4. Target audience.** The framework supports up to 3 distinct audience segments. For the auto shop, the primary audience is middle-class Atlanta area residents who own a vehicle. Businesses with multiple product lines, service tiers, or geographic markets can add additional audience segments that each get their own targeting strategy.

## The 5 Components a Complete Strategy Covers

Here is exactly what a complete strategy addresses, and why leaving any component out creates a gap that limits what the other four can accomplish.

**Component 1: Google Ads.** Campaign structure, keyword targeting, bid strategy, match types, and negative keywords. This is where most businesses start. It captures high-intent buyers who are actively searching for what you offer. For the auto shop, these are people searching "auto repair near me" or "brake service Atlanta." [Google Ads](/digital-advertising/google-ads/) produces direct-response results at the bottom of the funnel, and it is the fastest path to trackable ROI for most local service businesses.

**Component 2: Facebook Ads.** Creative strategy, audience targeting, and funnel-level messaging. Paid social runs parallel to paid search, not instead of it. [Meta Ads](/digital-advertising/meta-ads/) reach buyers earlier in the decision process, when they are not yet searching but are part of the right demographic. For the auto shop, that means targeting the right geography and household profile before the driver even realizes their next service is overdue. The targeting logic is different from search, but both feed the same funnel.

**Component 3: SEO.** Long-term organic visibility through technical SEO, on-page optimization, and content. Paid ads produce results now. SEO builds compounding results over time. A strategy with paid ads and no SEO is running in place: the moment you stop spending, the visibility disappears. SEO changes that equation. It's the component most businesses add last, but it belongs in the plan from the start.

**Component 4: Full-funnel planning.** This is where the first three components get connected. Top of funnel, middle of funnel, and bottom of funnel each require different messaging, different creative, and different calls to action. According to Creekside Marketing's demonstrated framework, the strategy output addresses all three stages specifically, not generically.

**Component 5: Retention and upsells.** This is the component most paid ads agencies never touch. Retention covers how to keep clients after the first sale so turnover stays low. Recapture covers how to bring back existing clients and upsell them on additional services. For the auto shop, that means getting the customer back for every subsequent repair, not just the first one they found through search.

If you are only running acquisition ads with no retention layer, every new customer costs the same to acquire, forever. A retention strategy makes each customer more valuable over time and reduces the pressure on your acquisition budget to do all the heavy lifting.

## Full-Funnel Thinking: TOFU, MOFU, BOFU, and What Comes After

The funnel framework exists because the same message does not work for a buyer who has never heard of you and a buyer who is ready to book today. Different stages need different approaches.

**Top of funnel:** Building awareness with people who do not know you yet. Brand visibility, educational content, geographic reach. For the auto shop, this means putting the brand in front of the right Atlanta-area households before they need a repair.

**Middle of funnel:** Engaging buyers who know they have a problem and are evaluating options. Service-specific ads, comparison messaging, and content that answers "why should I choose you." Someone who has searched "best auto shop near me" twice this week is at MOFU.

**Bottom of funnel:** Capturing buyers who are ready to act. High-intent keywords, direct offers, clear calls to action. This is where Google Ads performs best for most local service businesses.

**After the funnel:** According to Creekside Marketing's full framework, the strategy does not end at the bottom of the funnel. Retention and recapture planning begins the moment the first client is won. The goal is to turn a one-time repair into a long-term customer relationship. This section of the strategy output specifically addresses how to reduce turnover and how to upsell existing clients on additional services as the relationship grows.

## The Piecemeal Implementation Approach

Here is a reality most complete strategy documents ignore: most businesses cannot implement everything at once. Budget constraints are real. Team capacity is real. For a local service business, running Google Ads, Meta Ads, and an SEO program simultaneously from day one is not always financially viable.

The answer is not to wait until you can afford everything at once. The answer is to sequence it.

According to Creekside Marketing's approach to implementation, you do not need to pay for or execute the entire strategy immediately. You can implement specific portions first, in priority order, get campaigns running, and then use some of the campaign profits and roll them over into additional strategies as the business grows.

In practice: start with the highest-ROI channel for your business type. For most local service businesses, that is Google Ads. Get it profitable. Use a portion of those profits to fund the next layer, whether that is Meta Ads, an SEO content program, or a retention campaign. Each phase funds the next.

This approach turns a complete strategy from an overwhelming document into an ordered roadmap. You know where you are going. You are implementing it in priority order. The business is growing its marketing investment in line with its marketing returns, not front-loading spend before results are proven.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much do I need to know about a business to build a complete strategy?**

Less than most people think. According to Creekside Marketing's workflow, the business's own website typically provides enough to complete all four strategy inputs. The services page covers what they offer, the about page covers differentiators, and the target audience is usually evident from the business type and location. You do not need weeks of discovery calls before building a real strategy.

**What if my budget only covers one or two channels?**

Start with the highest-ROI channel for your business type and implement the strategy for that channel first. Use a portion of the returns to fund the next phase. The complete strategy is a roadmap, not a requirement to do everything at once.

**Does the framework work if I have multiple target audiences?**

Yes. The framework supports up to 3 distinct audience segments. Each gets its own targeting strategy within the broader plan. Businesses with different service tiers, geographies, or customer types can address each audience specifically instead of using a single generic message.

**What is the difference between the full-funnel approach and just running bottom-of-funnel ads?**

Bottom-of-funnel ads capture buyers who are already ready to act. They work well but only reach a fraction of the available market. A full-funnel approach also captures buyers earlier in the decision process, builds brand awareness, and creates a pipeline so you are not entirely dependent on demand that already exists. Over time, a full-funnel strategy produces compounding results that bottom-of-funnel-only campaigns cannot match.

**What does the retention layer actually include?**

Retention covers everything that happens after the first sale: strategies to keep clients coming back, methods to reduce turnover, and upsell approaches for additional services. For a service business, this is often the highest-margin part of the entire strategy because the cost to retain an existing client is almost always lower than the cost to acquire a new one.
