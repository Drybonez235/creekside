Here's the thing about Google Ads app exclusions: Google defaults every account to show ads across mobile applications, and they charge you for every click. The person tapping your HVAC ad inside a mobile game is not calling for a quote. The person who clicks your dental offer in a kids' puzzle app is not booking a consult.

For most of the accounts we manage, app placements are waste. Cleaning them up is one of the fastest wins in any Google Ads audit.

## What App Placements Actually Are in Google Ads

App placements are impressions and clicks that happen inside mobile apps rather than on websites or search results. Google's Display Network reaches apps across both the Play Store and App Store ecosystems, and every new campaign is eligible to show there unless you explicitly block it. That eligibility is on by default, not something you opt into.

It is not a niche issue. Any campaign that touches Display inventory is in scope. Search campaigns with Display Network expansion enabled (a default setting Google frequently recommends or pre-selects during campaign setup), Performance Max campaigns, and standard Display campaigns all reach app inventory. The spend is real, and the conversion rate on app traffic for non-app-focused campaigns is almost always lower than on search or site-based placements.

Google's incentive here is not aligned with yours. They get paid per click regardless of whether the lead converts. According to Creekside Marketing's account audit work across clients in $20M+ of managed spend, app placement traffic routinely appears in placement reports with meaningful spend and near-zero conversion activity. You only benefit when a click converts. That gap is why app exclusions exist, and why applying them manually is your job, not Google's.

## The 140+ App Exclusions Problem

There are over 140 app categories you can exclude in Google Ads. Manually reviewing all of them, deciding which apply, and applying exclusions is genuinely painful. Not because the concept is hard to understand. Because the interface gives you no shortcut.

You navigate to Placements, open Exclusions, and start working through a list. Each category requires its own action. For anyone managing a handful of accounts that is annoying. For anyone managing 20 or 30 accounts it is a serious time drain, and the outcome is zero campaign improvement. No creative insight, no bid optimization, no strategic learning. Just clean-up work Google could have designed to be easier and did not.

The default is that Google continues serving your ads in apps and collecting the revenue. You have to actively opt out. That asymmetry is the core problem here.

## Which Google Ads Accounts Should Exclude App Placements?

A good majority of accounts would benefit from excluding app categories, but not every single account should exclude all of them. That distinction matters before you paste in a blanket exclusion list.

| Account Type | Worth Keeping App Traffic? |
|, -|, -|
| Local service businesses (HVAC, roofing, dental) | No. Exclude. |
| Lead generation (attorneys, financial services, B2B) | No. Exclude. |
| Ecommerce running broad Display | Sometimes. Check placement report first. |
| App install campaigns | Yes. This is the entire purpose. |
| Brand awareness with large budgets and reach goals | Sometimes intentional. Evaluate. |

If your goal is phone calls, form fills, or any direct lead action, app traffic is almost always noise. The click happens accidentally or out of boredom inside a game, not out of purchase intent. Exclude it.

If you are running a campaign specifically designed to get app installs or broad brand impressions at scale, app placements serve a real function. That is the exception in most client portfolios, not the rule.

## How to Exclude App Placements in Google Ads (The Manual Method)

The native exclusion process runs through Placement Exclusions at the campaign level or through an account-level Placement Exclusion List that applies across multiple campaigns.

**Campaign-level exclusions:**
1. Open the campaign in Google Ads
2. Click Placements in the left sidebar
3. Select the Exclusions tab
4. Click the plus icon to add exclusions
5. Search for and select the app categories you want to block

**Account-level exclusions (better at scale):**
1. In Google Ads, go to Tools and Settings
2. Under Shared Library, open "Placement exclusion lists"
3. Create a new list, add your app category exclusions
4. Apply the list to all relevant campaigns

The account-level list is the better approach for any multi-campaign setup. You build it once, apply it everywhere, and updates propagate automatically. The problem with both paths is identical: working through 140+ categories manually takes time you could spend on higher-value optimization.

## A Few Things to Know Before You Apply

Before you paste the full exclusion list into every account, here is a short checklist. Not every account has identical inventory exposure, and Performance Max handles placements differently from standard campaigns. Reviewing these four points first will save you from accidentally blocking traffic you actually wanted.

**Pull your placement report first.** In Google Ads, you can see which specific apps are actually receiving impressions and clicks. If you see a particular app eating spend with no conversions, that is your strongest signal that exclusions are warranted. The spreadsheet method handles the broad category sweep. Your placement report catches the specific offenders you can also add as individual exclusions.

**Performance Max is more limited.** Account-level placement exclusion lists do apply to PMax campaigns, but the campaign-level exclusion interface inside PMax is more restricted than standard Display or Search campaigns. After applying your account-level list, verify it is actually registered and active against your PMax campaigns.

**This does not replace negative keyword review.** App exclusions address where your ads show. Negative keywords address what search queries trigger them. Both matter. Exclusions without an ongoing search term review still leaves waste in the account.

**Build it into your new account checklist.** Every account we set up gets an exclusion pass in the first week. Not glamorous. Reliable. Quarterly review after that is usually sufficient, especially with an auto-updating list. Our full [Google Ads campaign settings checklist](/blog/google-ads-campaign-settings-checklist/) includes app and placement exclusions as part of the standard new-account setup sequence.

## FAQ

**Does this apply to my Google Search campaigns?**
If your Search campaign has Display Network expansion enabled, yes. Google often recommends enabling this setting during campaign creation, which opens up Display and app inventory. Disabling expansion or applying app exclusions will affect that traffic. A Search campaign set to search-only with no Display expansion is largely unaffected.

**Will excluding apps hurt my Performance Max results?**
It may reduce impression volume slightly. For lead gen and service business accounts, that trade is almost always worth it. Fewer impressions from low-intent app traffic is not a loss. Check your placement report after applying exclusions and look at what changed in the first two to three weeks.

**How do I know if my account is currently spending on apps?**
In Google Ads, go to Placements under Content, then look at where your Display and PMax inventory is going. Filter for mobile app entries. If you see app placements with meaningful spend and low conversion volume, you have your answer.

**Can I exclude specific apps instead of whole categories?**
Yes. You can add specific app bundle IDs to an exclusion list alongside or instead of category-level exclusions. Category exclusions are the fastest starting point. Individual app exclusions let you get precise about repeat offenders once you have placement data to work from.

**How quickly do exclusions take effect?**
Google typically applies placement exclusions within a few hours. Full propagation across all campaigns in your account may take up to 24 hours. Pull your placement report a few days later to confirm the expected apps are no longer showing up.
