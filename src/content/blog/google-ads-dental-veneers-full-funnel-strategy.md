Most dental practices running Google Ads for veneers are running a single Search campaign with a $3,000 to $5,000 monthly budget, and wondering why returns are inconsistent. The missing piece is almost never the keywords. It is the funnel architecture around the keywords.

We manage Google Ads for dental veneers at a high-end cosmetic practice in California where each patient case is worth approximately $20,000. In the past 30 days, the account produced 7 booked appointments and roughly $140,000 in directly attributed revenue on $13,000 in combined ad spend. That works out to about an 11x ROAS, and it does not come from a single clever Search campaign. It comes from three campaign types working together in a structure most practices have never set up.

Here is exactly how the account is built, what the numbers look like, and the two findings that should change how you approach Google Ads for high-ticket dental services.

## Why a Dental Veneer Practice Needs a Full-Funnel Google Ads Strategy

A full funnel is necessary for Google Ads for dental veneers because the purchase decision is slow, the patient pool in any given market is limited, and a single-channel Search-only approach starves Google of the behavioral data it needs to optimize effectively. At $20,000 per case, patients research for weeks or months before they book. If your ads only appear at the Search stage, you are competing exclusively for people already close to a decision, paying the highest CPCs, against every other practice in your market.

The other reason a full funnel matters here is attribution complexity. When a patient has seen a YouTube ad, then searched the practice name, then converted through a Search campaign, attributing the result only to Search understates the role upper-funnel campaigns play in getting that patient to search in the first place. According to Creekside Marketing's direct account analysis, we track booked appointments rather than just form submissions or call clicks as the primary conversion event. One booked appointment for a veneer practice means $20,000. That single fact changes how every dollar of ad spend should be evaluated.

## How the Google Ads Account for Dental Veneers Is Structured

The account runs on three campaign types: Performance Max handles hybrid demand and display reach, Search captures high-intent queries at the conversion stage, and Demand Gen drives top-of-funnel awareness on YouTube at very low CPMs. Each tier has a defined role. They are not competing with each other. They are compounding.

**Performance Max with Two Asset Groups**

Rather than one broad Performance Max campaign, we run two distinct asset groups inside a single campaign. One asset group is built around porcelain veneer searches, using broader audience signals and 30 search themes. The other targets no prep veneers specifically, using tighter targeting and 6 search themes.

The distinction matters because no prep veneers represent a higher-intent search. Someone typing "no prep veneers" has already done meaningful research. They know what the procedure involves. That specificity translates to less advertiser competition, lower CPCs, and a conversion rate that is approximately equal to or better than the broader porcelain veneer group, despite the narrower audience.

One detail worth noting about the health and advertising policy environment: every image in this account has been flagged by Google due to health and personalized advertising policy violations. We are not showing images in Performance Max. The account is still generating these results. Policy violations in the dental space appear alarming, but based on Creekside Marketing's analysis across multiple health and dental accounts, they are frequently AI-generated false positives that do not prevent strong performance. They should be monitored and addressed where possible, but they should not be treated as campaign-ending problems.

**Search Campaigns**

The Search campaigns are the conversion engine. We use the same ad copy in Search that appears in Performance Max, which keeps messaging consistent and reduces the production overhead of building separate creative for each campaign type. The Search campaigns focus on capturing people who have shown the strongest intent signals for veneer treatments.

**Demand Gen on YouTube**

This is where approximately $2,000 per month goes toward showing up on YouTube in front of everyone within roughly a 40-mile radius of the practice. We target YouTube placements specifically and avoid display network placements entirely. The reasoning is straightforward: display networks carry significant risk of clickfarm traffic and low-quality placements that consume budget without generating real patient demand. YouTube, being a reputable platform with engaged viewers, produces impressions that actually matter.

According to Creekside Marketing's account data, the Demand Gen campaign is generating approximately 13x cheaper impressions than the next cheapest campaign type in the account, with clicks at roughly 50% of the cost of other campaigns. The Demand Gen campaign does not generate revenue by itself. It generates reach and recognition. That recognition effect raises the conversion rate in Search and Performance Max when those same people later search for veneer services, even when it cannot be directly attributed in the data.

## Broad Match Beat Phrase Match for Dental Veneer Keywords

Broad match keywords are dominating this account's Search performance. Phrase match is getting close to zero conversions. That surprises nearly everyone we share this finding with, because the conventional wisdom has long been that phrase match provides necessary control while broad match wastes budget. In this account, the conventional wisdom is wrong.

According to Creekside Marketing's analysis across multiple service-based accounts, broad match outperforms phrase match consistently when three conditions are met. First, you must be feeding Google high-quality conversion signals from your CRM, not just form submissions or click-to-call events. Second, you need an aggressive, maintained negative keyword list that cuts off irrelevant traffic before it converts. Third, you need enough monthly spend to give Google's algorithm the data volume it needs to identify which searchers actually book appointments.

In this account, all three conditions are in place. The result: Google's targeting improvements have made broad match more contextually precise than phrase match was several years ago. Broad match now adapts to the searcher's intent and surrounding behavioral signals rather than simply matching query strings. When you give it room to operate and keep it hemmed in with negatives, it finds converters that phrase match would miss entirely.

If you try to run broad match while feeding only click-based conversion data to Google, you will get poor results. Google will optimize for whatever you tell it to optimize for. If that signal is a form click, it will target people who click forms and never book. Connecting your CRM so that booked appointments flow back into Google Ads as offline conversions is not optional for this strategy to work. It is the foundation everything else is built on.

No prep veneer keywords on broad match are currently producing a lower cost per conversion than porcelain veneer keywords on any match type. That is partly due to less advertiser competition on no prep veneer queries, and partly because the searcher has already done their homework and is genuinely closer to a booking decision.

## The Real Numbers From the Last 30 Days

The numbers from this account are not hypothetical projections. According to Creekside Marketing's direct account data, the Search campaigns produced 6 booked appointments in the past 30 days on approximately $4,000 in spend. At $20,000 per appointment, that is $120,000 in revenue from $4,000 in Search spend. That is a 30x ROAS for the Search component alone.

When the full account is rolled up, including Performance Max, branded search, and Demand Gen, the total reaches 7 attributed booked appointments and approximately $140,000 in revenue on $13,000 in combined spend. The overall account ROAS drops from 30x to approximately 11x because Performance Max and Demand Gen carry overhead that does not always produce directly attributed conversions. That overhead is not waste. It is what makes a 30x Search ROAS achievable. The upper-funnel campaigns generate the demand that the Search campaigns convert.

There is also a practical ceiling on attribution in any account like this. Patients who saw a YouTube ad and later called the office directly, without ever clicking on another ad, do not show up in these figures. The $140,000 is a conservative floor, not a complete picture.

## The Creative Finding That Is Changing YouTube Performance

In the Demand Gen campaign, we have tested multiple video ad formats over time. The version that is currently outperforming every other iteration in this account is not a polished production. It is an authentic, lightly edited video featuring either a real patient discussing their experience or team members speaking directly to camera about the services.

Based on Creekside Marketing's direct observation in this account and others, highly produced and heavily edited video ads are underperforming relative to straightforward, human-focused content on YouTube. As the volume of AI-generated and template-produced content increases across platforms, viewers are becoming faster at recognizing corporate or formulaic creative and scrolling past it. A patient speaking genuinely about their veneer results, or the practice's team explaining the procedure in plain language, creates a real human connection. That connection makes a future Search conversion meaningfully more likely.

This is not a general principle applied from the outside. It is a direct observation from the account data, where the authentic video format has remained the top performer for the past month after being tested against multiple higher-production alternatives.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Google Ads for Dental Veneers

**How much should a dental veneer practice spend on Google Ads?**

A practice in a mid-to-large market can produce consistent returns starting at $4,000 to $6,000 per month in Search spend alone. Full-funnel accounts that include Performance Max and Demand Gen typically run between $10,000 and $20,000 per month depending on market competition and geographic radius. At $20,000 per patient case, a single booked appointment covers most monthly budgets. The math is unusually favorable compared to lower-ticket service businesses.

**Does Performance Max work for dental veneers?**

Yes, when structured with distinct asset groups targeting different intent levels. Lumping all veneer-related keywords into one asset group reduces control and typically increases cost per conversion. Separating porcelain veneer searches (higher volume, broader audience) from no prep veneer searches (lower volume, higher intent) allows Google to optimize each group independently and reach two meaningfully different segments of the target market.

**Why run Demand Gen if it does not generate direct revenue?**

Demand Gen on YouTube provides top-of-funnel awareness at a fraction of the CPM cost of Search or Performance Max. At approximately $2,000 per month, it generates impressions at roughly 13x lower cost than the next cheapest campaign in this account. The value is not in direct conversions. It is in priming the market so that when high-intent searchers eventually look for veneer services, they recognize the practice's name and are more likely to convert. Removing Demand Gen would likely depress Search performance even if the direct attribution data does not show the connection clearly.

**What do you do about Google's health advertising policy violations for dental practices?**

In this account, every image asset has been flagged by Google's health and personalized advertising policy, so no images are showing at all. The campaign is still generating strong results. Health policy violations in the dental space frequently come from automated detection systems and can affect compliant accounts. They are worth addressing through the policy appeal process, but they should not be treated as disqualifying. Ad copy and video assets can carry performance without image placements.

**How do you handle multi-channel attribution when running Google Ads and Facebook Ads simultaneously for dental veneers?**

This account runs Google Ads alongside Facebook Ads for the same practice. To avoid double-attributing a patient, we track booked appointments as the primary conversion event in both platforms and apply a conservative multi-channel view. If someone engaged with a Facebook ad and later converted through Google Search, we do not count two $20,000 appointments. We identify the appointment and allocate attribution based on the full conversion path. Without this discipline, reported ROAS figures across both platforms would overstate actual revenue generated.
