# Why a Mechanical Engineer and an MBA Handle Your Paid Ads Differently Than Most Agencies

This post is based on a video Peterson published on the Creekside Marketing YouTube channel: [Upwork Profile Video, Samuel and Cade](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lY6uxRje3mk).

Most people hiring a paid ads specialist expect a marketer. What they get with us is different: a mechanical engineer running [Google Ads](/digital-advertising/google-ads/) and an MBA with an accounting background running [Meta Ads](/digital-advertising/meta-ads/). After four years running campaigns together and auditing more than 200 accounts, we are convinced that background is not a quirk. It is the reason we diagnose accounts differently than agencies staffed with pure marketers.

Here is what that actually looks like inside a paid ads account, and why it matters for your ROI.

---

## The Problem With Hiring the Wrong Paid Ads Specialist

If someone tells you they manage Google Ads, Meta Ads, TikTok, SEO, CRO, and analytics, that is not a feature. It is a warning sign that their attention is divided across platforms that each require deep, dedicated expertise. Google Ads and Meta Ads have different auction mechanics, bidding systems, creative requirements, and optimization levers. Managing both well requires two different mindsets.

As Peterson puts it directly: "If you go with someone who says they're a jack of all trades, typically that means master of none." That observation comes from auditing hundreds of accounts. The pattern is consistent: generalists miss platform-specific details that specialists catch immediately, because specialists live inside the mechanics of one platform. A campaign structure that works on Google because of intent-based search fails on Meta when someone applies Google bidding logic to cold-traffic audiences.

Our operation is built around specialization by design. Peterson handles Google Ads. Cade handles Meta Ads. Four years of working that way has produced a well-oiled coordination system where each platform gets someone who thinks about nothing else.

---

## What a Mechanical Engineering Background Changes in Google Ads

Mechanical engineering trains you to isolate variables, diagnose root causes, and confirm a hypothesis before taking action. Those habits transfer directly into Google Ads account management in ways that pure marketing training often does not produce.

Peterson describes getting into marketing precisely because of that numbers orientation: "we actually both got into marketing because we're incredibly good with numbers and we found that that's something that a lot of marketing agencies lack is the ability to find relevant data and find relevant insights in their marketing campaigns to improve upon them." That insight comes from reviewing more than 200 accounts and seeing the same analytical failures repeat.

The engineering background shows up most directly in conversion tracking. Google's Smart Bidding relies entirely on the conversion data it receives. If that data is wrong, the algorithm optimizes toward the wrong goal. Peterson's approach treats [conversion tracking](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-settings/) as a system with inputs and outputs: verify every input before trusting any output, and build the tracking foundation before any campaign goes live.

As Peterson puts it: "If you've bad data, you're going to have bad results." This is a literal system problem. We use Google Tag Manager for conversion tracking on both Google Ads and Meta Ads, and verify every tag setup before launch. That step alone eliminates a class of performance problems we see repeatedly in audited accounts.

## The MBA Accounting Mind on Meta Ads

An accounting background on Meta Ads produces the same advantage: structured data review, process discipline, and a paper trail for every decision rather than improvised optimization based on gut feel.

Cade holds an MBA and comes from an accounting major background. On Meta, this shows up in the audit process. When a new client comes on, Cade runs through a comprehensive checklist to evaluate the structural state of the account before making a single change. The audit identifies what is wrong, what needs to change, and in what priority order. The output is a 90-day plan with specific implementation steps, not vague recommendations.

The ongoing optimization work follows the same discipline. Cade describes it this way: "I have this giant checklist of things that I do daily and sometimes twice a day, sometimes two times a week, where I make all the notes needed to leave a paper trail for your account." For a business owner trying to understand what their Meta Ads manager is actually doing between calls, that kind of documented activity log is rare. Most agencies report outcomes. This documents what changed and why, every day.

Specialization only produces results if the two specialists coordinate effectively. After four years managing Google and Meta accounts for the same clients, we have built a system that keeps both platforms aligned without one stepping on the other.

We work with two types of clients: agencies that white-label our services, and business owners who hire us directly. For agency clients, we operate as the behind-the-scenes platform specialists. For direct clients, we run both accounts and report directly to the business owner. In both arrangements, the structure is the same: Peterson owns Google, Cade owns Meta, and both platforms get full-time attention from someone whose entire focus is that platform.

Creekside Marketing works with both large agencies like Duct Tape Marketing and smaller businesses across a range of industries. See [client results across both platforms](/case-study-digital-marketing/). Based on that experience, the specialist model consistently outperforms the generalist model because the things that matter most on each platform are genuinely different. Google Ads rewards keyword structure, bidding precision, and conversion data quality. Meta Ads rewards creative iteration, audience segmentation, and funnel architecture. Neither skill set transfers cleanly to the other.

## The Four-Step Onboarding That Sets Accounts Up to Succeed

A defined onboarding process produces consistent results across every new account relationship. Random check-ins and informal handoffs do not. Here is the four-step structure we use when a new paid ads client comes on board:

**Step 1: Account Audit.** Before any changes are made, we run through the existing account using a comprehensive checklist. This surfaces structural problems: misaligned campaign objectives, targeting issues, creative gaps, and tracking discrepancies. The audit shows exactly what needs to change and in what order.

**Step 2: 90-Day Plan.** Based on the audit, we build a 90-day roadmap specifying what we are implementing, in what sequence, and what results to expect. The client gets a concrete plan instead of open-ended promises.

**Step 3: Onboarding Document.** New clients receive an onboarding document covering everything needed to get started: how to share account access, how tracking is set up, and what to expect from reporting. The account belongs to the client from the beginning.

**Step 4: Ongoing Optimization Log.** Every account gets a running log of daily and weekly optimization actions. What changed, when, and why. This is the paper trail described above, maintained for the life of the engagement.

This structure reflects the same instinct behind the specialist model: a defined process scales. An informal one does not.

## The Transparency Non-Negotiable: Clients Own Their Accounts

One operating principle shapes everything else: clients always have full access to the accounts we manage, and they own those accounts outright.

Peterson has seen the alternative during account audits, and it is common: "Some people have gotten screwed in the past by agencies, by freelancers that basically hold their work hostage." This means restricted account access, proprietary tracking structures the client cannot move, and reporting dashboards that summarize without revealing. When that agency relationship ends, the client often loses the account history entirely.

We operate the opposite way. Every client has direct access to the ad accounts we build and manage. The reporting is designed so the client can see inside the account, not just summary numbers. If a client decides to move on, the account moves with them. Nothing is held hostage.

This is a structural decision, not a marketing promise. Clients who can see inside their accounts ask better questions, catch discrepancies faster, and build a clearer picture of what their advertising investment is actually doing. That transparency also creates natural accountability on our side: we cannot hide behind opaque dashboards when the client can pull up the same data we see.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between a paid ads specialist and a generalist marketing agency?**
A paid ads specialist focuses exclusively on one advertising platform, building deep expertise in its auction mechanics, bidding systems, and optimization levers. A generalist agency divides attention across multiple platforms and disciplines. After auditing 200+ accounts at Creekside Marketing, the specialist approach consistently produces better results, particularly for accounts with significant monthly spend where platform-specific precision matters most.

**Why does an educational background in engineering or accounting help in paid advertising?**
Most paid ads failures are data problems, not creative problems. Engineering and accounting backgrounds train analytical thinking: isolating variables, reading data critically, and confirming a diagnosis before taking action. Peterson's mechanical engineering background and Cade's MBA and accounting training apply that same discipline to paid ad management, which produces better diagnostic decisions than generic marketing experience alone.

**What does conversion tracking have to do with paid ads performance?**
Conversion tracking is the data foundation Google's Smart Bidding uses to optimize campaign targeting and bids. If tracking is incorrect, whether from spam conversions, wrong trigger events, or tag misfires, the algorithm optimizes toward the wrong goal. Fixing tracking before a campaign launches is one of the highest-leverage improvements in most accounts. We use Google Tag Manager for both platforms and verify every setup before going live.

**What should a new paid ads client expect in the first 90 days?**
Based on Creekside Marketing's onboarding process: a comprehensive account audit, a 90-day implementation plan with specific milestones, an onboarding document covering access and tracking setup, and a running daily and weekly optimization log that documents every change made to the account.

**Can one team manage both Google Ads and Meta Ads effectively?**
Yes, when the team is structured around specialists rather than generalists. Peterson handles Google Ads exclusively and Cade handles Meta Ads exclusively. Both platforms receive full-time attention from someone whose focus is that platform. For clients running both, the two accounts do not compete for a single manager's time.

**How do you keep multiple client accounts separate?**
Creekside Marketing uses a management system that tracks each client account, the agencies those clients operate under, and what actions have been taken inside each account. Each client is kept completely separate in terms of access, tracking, and reporting. The running optimization log is also part of how individual account histories stay organized over the life of the engagement.
