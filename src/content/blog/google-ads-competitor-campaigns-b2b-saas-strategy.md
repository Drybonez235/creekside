# The Competitor Campaign Mistake Every B2B SaaS Client Makes in Google Ads (And What to Do Instead)

Every B2B SaaS client we bring on makes the same request within the first week of working together: "Can we run competitor campaigns in Google Ads?"

The logic feels airtight. If someone is searching for a competitor by name, they are in-market for software in that category. Get in front of them with a compelling headline and you steal the lead before they ever hit the competitor's landing page.

The problem is the logic breaks down in practice. Based on our experience running Google Ads for B2B SaaS companies, this intuition leads most clients into one of the most expensive early mistakes they can make.

---

## Why the Competitor Campaign Instinct Makes Sense But Usually Fails

Competitor campaigns rarely work for new B2B SaaS Google Ads accounts because they put the hardest conversion challenge first. If your landing page, offer, and campaign structure cannot convert unbiased in-market searchers, targeting someone already looking for a specific competitor will not compensate for those gaps. Prove generic search works, then expand.

The appeal of Google Ads competitor campaigns is understandable. A searcher typing a competitor's brand name is clearly not a cold prospect. They know the category exists, they know at least one player in the space, and they have enough intent to search. That reads like a warm audience.

Here is the counterintuitive issue: if you cannot convince people who have no existing brand preference to buy your product, there is no reason to believe you can convince someone actively searching for a specific competitor.

Generic search terms are where buyers with no brand loyalty land. They type "B2B CRM for small teams" or "project management software for agencies." They have not made up their minds. They are genuinely open. And if your landing page, offer, and campaign structure cannot convert that audience, you are trying to win a harder fight with a broken weapon when you go after competitor traffic.

According to Creekside Marketing's experience across B2B SaaS accounts, generic search is where accounts prove whether the fundamentals work. Competitor campaigns should extend a working system, not substitute for one that has not been tested yet.

---

## The Login-Intent Problem That Kills Competitor Campaign ROI

Pure brand keyword targeting in Google Ads carries a hidden waste problem that erodes competitor campaign ROI from day one. A significant portion of brand name searches come from existing customers navigating to the login page, users who can never become your customer. When they click your ad by mistake, you pay for a permanently unqualifiable lead.

There is a second structural problem with pure brand keyword targeting that most advertisers never think about until they have already wasted budget on it.

A meaningful portion of anyone searching for a brand name by itself is an existing customer navigating to the login page.

Think about your own daily behavior. You open a new tab, type "Salesforce" or "HubSpot," and head straight to log in. You are not in the market. You are not evaluating alternatives. You are just a user trying to access a tool you already pay for.

That traffic shows up in your competitor campaign. Someone might click your ad, because it is sitting above the organic result, land on your page, realize it is not what they wanted, and leave immediately. You paid for that click. The person was never a prospect. They were never going to become your customer under any reasonable circumstance.

Peterson Rainey, founder of Creekside Marketing: "People searching for the brand name may be current customers looking to login, meaning they will never click on your ad, and if they do you just lost money."

This is one of the cleaner examples of structural paid ads waste we see. The impression volume looks encouraging. The click-through rate on a strong ad might even be decent. But a portion of that traffic pool is permanently unqualifiable, and that contaminates the data you are relying on to judge performance.

## The Competitor Alternative Campaign: A Better Strategy

The right way to run competitor campaigns in Google Ads is to filter for a more specific intent signal: people who are actively looking to switch away from or evaluate against a specific competitor. Not their full user base. Not people logging in. People who are already dissatisfied or already comparing.

The keyword strategy that captures this is the competitor alternative campaign. Instead of targeting "LinkedIn" or "Salesforce," you target:

- "LinkedIn alternatives"
- "LinkedIn vs Salesforce Navigator"
- "best LinkedIn alternative for small teams"
- "switch from HubSpot"
- "HubSpot alternative 2025"

These searchers are categorically different from general brand searchers. They are not logging into anything. They are not renewing a subscription. They have a specific dissatisfaction with a competitor or they are actively comparing it against other options. That is the intent signal worth buying.

As Peterson Rainey puts it from experience with B2B SaaS accounts: this strategy "allows you to filter for people actively interested in the products your competitor offers, but who aren't already captured by that brand."

The result is a narrower but much more actionable audience. You spend less on login-intent waste and more on buyers who actually have a reason to consider switching.

## The Right Order of Operations for B2B SaaS Google Ads

The sequencing principle behind this is straightforward. Win on generic search first. Then extend into competitor alternative targeting.

When you launch a Google Ads campaign for a B2B SaaS product, the first job is to establish that the funnel converts. You need evidence that when an unbiased, in-market buyer lands on your page, a meaningful percentage of them take action. That evidence comes from generic search terms, where people are searching for the solution you sell without a specific brand in mind.

Once generic search terms are converting at an acceptable cost per acquisition, you have something to build on. You have proof that the offer resonates, that the landing page works, and that the campaign structure can generate leads. At that point, expanding into competitor alternative keywords is a logical next move. You are applying a working system to an adjacent, highly motivated audience.

This is the opposite of what most B2B SaaS companies want to do. They want to start with competitors because it feels more aggressive, more targeted. But running competitor campaigns before your funnel is proven means you are spending money to find out why your offer does not convert, using the most skeptical audience possible.

We tell every new B2B SaaS client the same thing: you should not run competitor campaigns until you have been successful with generic search terms first. This is not a conservative take. It is the approach that produces accounts with sustainable growth, rather than accounts that spend aggressively in month one and then go quiet because nothing converted.

## When Competitor Campaigns Do Make Sense Earlier

Competitor campaigns make sense earlier in the lifecycle when your brand has clear alternative positioning, a comparison landing page built for the specific competitor, and audience exclusions in place to filter existing customers. These three conditions change the math significantly. Without all three, earlier launch typically burns budget faster without producing better data.

There are specific situations where launching competitor campaigns earlier in the campaign lifecycle makes sense, even before generic search is fully proven.

**Clear, direct alternative positioning.** If your product is known in the market as the direct alternative to a specific competitor and your whole positioning is built around that comparison, the intent match is stronger. Buyers searching for that competitor may already know you exist as an option. That changes the calculus.

**A dedicated comparison landing page.** The competitor alternative strategy works best when the landing page speaks directly to the comparison. A generic homepage rarely closes this traffic. A page built around "Why teams switch from [Competitor] to [Your Product]" with specific differentiators converts at a meaningfully higher rate.

**Audience exclusions in place.** More sophisticated accounts run competitor campaigns with audience exclusions that filter out recent site visitors who have already converted, current customers, and remarketing lists of existing users. This setup significantly reduces the login-intent waste problem but requires additional configuration that most new campaigns do not have in place at launch.

Even in these cases, the competitor alternative keyword approach, targeting "X alternatives" and "X vs Y" type queries, consistently outperforms pure brand terms because of the intent clarity advantage.

## A Note on Conversion Tracking Before Any of This

None of this sequencing matters if your conversion tracking is broken or pointed at the wrong events. Generic search will look like it is not converting when it is. Competitor campaigns will look like they are working when they are not.

Before launching any campaign structure in Google Ads, make sure your [conversion tracking settings are correctly configured](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-settings) and that you are measuring actual downstream outcomes, not just form submissions from bots or event fires that do not represent real intent. The competitor campaign question is downstream of getting your measurement right.

## What This Looks Like Across a Campaign Lifecycle

A practical B2B SaaS Google Ads structure that follows this principle looks like this:

**Launch (months 1 to 3):** Generic search campaigns using intent-specific keywords that describe the problem your product solves. Tight campaign structure. Conversion tracking verified. Goal is proof that the funnel works at an acceptable CPL.

**Expansion (month 3 and beyond):** Competitor alternative campaigns added alongside the working generic structure. Keywords are intent-specific, not pure brand terms. Landing pages speak to the comparison, not to the generic product. Budget is calibrated to the proven CPL benchmark from generic search.

**Later evaluation:** Pure brand competitor campaigns, if they come into the plan at all, are evaluated case by case based on specific competitive dynamics, audience exclusion capabilities, and the account's demonstrated performance at that stage.

This is not the most immediately exciting pitch to give a client who wants to go head-to-head with competitors from day one. But it is the structure that produces accounts that scale without burning budget on traffic that cannot convert.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it a problem if competitors run campaigns against us?**
No. Let them. Defending against competitor campaigns is much less valuable than building a campaign structure that wins on genuine intent. If your generic search campaigns are converting at target cost, a competitor showing up in brand searches against you is not a significant threat.

**Can competitor alternative campaigns run from launch in some cases?**
Yes, in specific cases. If your entire brand positioning is built around being a direct alternative to a specific competitor, and you have a landing page built for that comparison, competitor alternative campaigns can launch alongside generic search from day one. What we recommend waiting on is pure brand keyword targeting, not alternative query targeting.

**How do I know when generic search is performing well enough to expand?**
A useful benchmark is a stable cost per lead on generic search terms at or below your target customer acquisition cost over at least 30 days with meaningful volume. At that point, the funnel is proven and adding competitor alternative targeting is a logical, low-risk extension.

**Does this apply to Meta Ads as well?**
Meta operates on audience targeting, not search intent, so the competitor campaign question is primarily a Google Ads decision. That said, the core principle, prove that your funnel converts before expanding to harder-to-convert audiences, applies to Meta as well.

**What if our competitor has terrible reviews and we can capture dissatisfied users?**
This is exactly the right instinct, and it reinforces the competitor alternative strategy. Target "alternatives to X" queries, build a landing page that addresses the specific complaints you know customers have about the competitor, and make the switch easy. That is using competitor weakness strategically without burning budget on users who are still happy with the product.

## The Bottom Line

The instinct to run Google Ads competitor campaigns early is almost universal among B2B SaaS clients. In most cases, it leads to wasted budget on login-intent traffic, a broken feedback loop, and slow learning about what actually converts.
