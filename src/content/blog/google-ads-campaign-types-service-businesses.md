## Why Google Ads Campaign Types for Service Businesses Matter More Than You Think

For most service businesses, choosing the wrong Google Ads campaign type does not just waste budget. It produces bad data, trains Google's algorithm on the wrong signals, and makes optimization nearly impossible. The eight campaign types Google offers each serve a specific purpose, and only one of them is the right starting point for a service business with an active account manager in the seat.

This post breaks down all eight campaign types, explains what they actually do, and gives you the exact decision framework we use when setting up new accounts.

## Search Campaigns: The Default for Almost Every Service Business

Search campaigns are Google Ads at its most fundamental: your ad shows at the top of Google when someone searches for a keyword you are targeting. The intent behind that search is the entire point. You are not interrupting someone doing something else. You are showing up at the exact moment they are looking for what you offer.

According to Creekside Marketing's analysis across $20M+ in managed ad spend, Search campaigns are the starting point for most service businesses because they give you the most control. You control which keywords trigger your ad, what match types you use, how much you bid, what devices you target, and which placements you include or exclude. That control is what makes optimization possible.

Inside Search campaigns, you can optionally enable two add-ons:

- **Search Partners:** Shows your ads on non-Google websites that use Google's search technology, as well as Google properties like Gmail and YouTube
- **Display Network:** Shows your ads as banner placements across the web alongside your search results

We typically start with Search only, accumulate data, and then evaluate whether enabling Search Partners or Display adds performance. The critical difference between Search and Performance Max is that inside a Search campaign, you can turn these options off if they are not performing. That optionality is why Search is the right starting point for accounts with active management.

## Performance Max: What It Is and Why It Is Not Our First Choice

Performance Max is Google's all-in-one campaign type. It runs across Search, Search Partners, Display, YouTube, Discover, Gmail, and Maps simultaneously. Google's algorithm decides where to serve your ads, to whom, and at what time.

According to Creekside Marketing's analysis, Performance Max functions as a less controlled version of a Search campaign. It does everything a Search campaign does, and more, but you cannot turn off Search Partners or Display inside it. You cannot isolate what is working by channel. Google makes those decisions, and your data is aggregated across all placements.

That said, Performance Max is genuinely the right choice in one specific situation: when you do not have the time or expertise to manage a campaign actively. If you are a business owner running your own ads with limited time, Performance Max requires fewer settings to configure, and Google will generate copy for you. The tradeoff is transparency. If you are not regularly reviewing the account, that tradeoff is acceptable.

For a deeper look at how we structure Performance Max when we do use it, see our guide on [how to set up Performance Max signals so Google gets the right data](/blog/performance-max-signals-setup-framework/).

## Display Campaigns: Only After You Have Proved It in Search First

A Display-only campaign targets banner placements across the web. We do not recommend starting with Display. According to Creekside Marketing's account management process, we create a Display-only campaign only after we have seen Display placements perform inside an existing Search campaign first.

The logic is data-driven: when you run a Search campaign with the Display Network enabled, you can monitor which channel, Search or Display, is actually generating results. If Display starts to outperform your other placements significantly, that is the signal to break it out into its own campaign and concentrate budget there. You are following data, not guessing.

Creating a Display-only campaign from the start, before you have that baseline, is speculative spending. Display typically generates cheaper clicks and more volume than Search, but with lower intent behind those clicks. That can look good on surface metrics while performing poorly on actual conversions.

## Shopping Campaigns: Reserved for Ecommerce Only

Shopping campaigns show product images with price and retailer information directly in search results. They are purpose-built for ecommerce businesses that sell physical products online. If you do not sell products from your website, there is nothing here for you and this campaign type should not be in your account.

According to Creekside Marketing's analysis, Shopping campaigns are reserved for brands that actually sell products online because the targeting logic is built around purchase intent for specific items. It does not map to service-based lead generation or appointment scheduling. Running a Shopping campaign for a service business burns budget on intent signals that cannot convert.

## Video Campaigns: For Promoting YouTube Content

Video campaigns run ads on YouTube and Google video partner sites. If you have a YouTube video you want to put in front of viewers while they browse, this is the campaign type for it.

Two practical constraints apply from our experience. First, the source video needs to be well-produced and well-edited. You are asking someone to stop what they are doing and watch something. Low production quality works against you from the first second. Second, the video needs to reach its point fast. Viewers have the option to skip after five seconds. According to Creekside Marketing's experience with video campaigns, if your video does not establish a compelling reason to keep watching in those first five seconds, most people will skip it before your message lands.

Shorter and more direct outperforms longer and comprehensive, because you retain more viewers through to the actual message.

## App Campaigns: A Narrow but Valid Use Case

App campaigns drive app downloads across Search, Display, YouTube, and the Google Play Store simultaneously. If raw download volume is your primary goal and you have a mobile app to promote, App campaigns are built for that specific objective.

One clarification worth knowing: you do not need an App campaign to promote app downloads. You can direct people to an app store page from a standard Search or Display campaign. App campaigns are specifically optimized for install volume, which matters if that metric is the core conversion goal. For most service businesses, this campaign type is not relevant.

## Smart Campaigns: Being Phased Out. Do Not Build On Them.

Smart campaigns are a highly automated, low-control campaign type originally designed for advertisers with minimal time or expertise. According to Creekside Marketing's analysis, Google has been discussing phasing out Smart campaigns, and that timeline has likely progressed since the source video this post is based on was recorded.

The performance profile is unpredictable. Smart campaigns show to a broad audience, generate lots of impressions and clicks, but rarely convert at a meaningful rate. As Peterson has described it: running Smart campaigns produces results more by luck than by any optimization you can actually control, similar to running a mismanaged campaign where you get volume but cannot direct where it goes. If you are considering Smart campaigns, Performance Max is a better option that at least gives you signal data to inform future decisions.

## Demand Gen: Outside Our Standard Account Stack

Demand Gen is Google's audience-targeted display and video campaign type, designed to function more like paid social advertising. It targets based on interests, behaviors, and in-market signals rather than active search queries.

We do not use Demand Gen as part of our standard account structure. Our focus is intent-based advertising for service businesses, and Search campaigns capture that intent far more efficiently than audience-based targeting. If you want to understand how Demand Gen can fit alongside Search and Performance Max in a multi-channel account, we have covered that in [this full-funnel Google Ads strategy guide](/blog/full-funnel-google-ads-strategy-search-pmax-demand-gen/).

## The Google Ads Campaign Type Decision Framework for Service Businesses

Here is the exact framework we use when setting up new accounts. The goal is always to match the campaign type to the business model and the level of active management available.

| Your Situation | Recommended Campaign Type |
|, , , , |, , , , , , , -|
| Service business, active management | Search |
| Service business, limited management time | Performance Max |
| Ecommerce, selling products online | Shopping |
| Promoting a YouTube video | Video |
| Display outperforming in an existing Search account | Display-only |
| App downloads as the primary conversion goal | App |
| Smart campaigns or Demand Gen | Skip or move to Performance Max |

According to Creekside Marketing's analysis, the most common mistake we see in new accounts is defaulting to Performance Max because the setup appears simpler, and then losing the ability to see what is actually driving results. Simpler to set up is not the same as simpler to optimize.

If you have an active manager on the account and you care about understanding performance at the keyword and placement level, start with Search. Add other campaign types after you have real data behind the decision. That sequencing is what enables proper [bid strategy progression as the account matures and eventually scales](/blog/google-ads-bid-strategy-progression-90-day-plan/).

## FAQ: Google Ads Campaign Types for Service Businesses

**Should I start with Performance Max or Search?**
For service businesses with an active account manager, Search is almost always the right starting point. You get keyword-level control, placement control, and the ability to see exactly what is working. Performance Max is the right choice when you cannot actively manage the account and need Google to handle more of the optimization decisions automatically.

**Can I run Search and Performance Max at the same time?**
Yes, and many accounts do. The key is making sure the two campaign types are not competing against each other on the same queries and that budgets reflect where actual performance is coming from. We typically establish Search first, let it prove performance, and then evaluate whether Performance Max adds incremental volume on top of it.

**Are Smart campaigns worth using?**
No, for most businesses. Smart campaigns offer minimal control, unpredictable results, and are being phased out by Google. If you want a campaign type that requires less active management, Performance Max is a better option with more data available to inform future decisions.

**When does a Display-only campaign make sense?**
Only after you have seen Display Network placements outperform other channels inside an existing Search campaign. Without that performance signal as a baseline, creating a Display-only campaign is speculative spending with nothing to build on.

**Do I need an App campaign to promote my app?**
No. Search and Display campaigns can drive traffic to an app store page. App campaigns are specifically optimized for install volume, which is the right choice if raw download count is the primary conversion goal. For most businesses, standard Search or Display targeting the right landing page is sufficient.
