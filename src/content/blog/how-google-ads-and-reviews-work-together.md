## How Google Ads and Google reviews work together

Ads get people to notice you. Reviews get them to choose you. That is the whole dynamic. Someone searches for your service, your ad appears, they click. Then they open a new tab. They check your Google reviews before they call. If your profile is strong, they call. If it is thin or outdated, they go back and click someone else. According to Creekside Marketing's work managing $20M+ in ad spend for local businesses, this is the sequence that determines whether paid traffic converts or leaks.

Running Google Ads without strong reviews means you are paying for clicks that land on an unverified profile. Running strong reviews without paid ads means slower top-of-funnel growth. The businesses winning local search do both.

## Why the ad alone does not close the deal

Here is a pattern we see constantly. A local business runs Google Ads, gets impressions, gets clicks, but the conversion rate stays low. The landing page is fine. The offer is fine. The problem is the tab they open after they click: a Google Business Profile with three reviews, the most recent from two years ago.

The prospect is not being irrational. They are doing exactly what you would do. Three reviews and silence tells them nothing about whether you show up on time, do good work, or answer the phone. So they go find someone with more signal.

Ads create the opportunity. Reviews validate it. You need both halves working.

## The 3-habit system for running ads and building reviews simultaneously

These three habits are what separate businesses that compound from businesses that stall. None of them require a marketing budget. They require consistency.

**Habit 1: Post to your Google Business Profile every week.**

Most local businesses post once, maybe twice, then forget about it. According to Creekside Marketing's analysis of local business profiles, the typical competitor posts to their Google Business Profile roughly once every three years and then quits. Google reads posting frequency as a signal of activity. Active businesses get shown more. Post a recent job, a new service or menu item, a tip, anything that shows Google the profile is live. Once a week is enough to be in the top tier of your local market on this signal alone.

**Habit 2: Respond to every review, good and bad.**

Thank positive reviewers by name. Stay calm and specific on negative ones. Do not argue, do not be defensive. The audience for your response is not the reviewer. It is every future customer who reads that exchange. Customers judge how you handle complaints more than they judge the complaint itself. A thoughtful public response to a bad review often builds more trust than a spotless record.

Google also reads response activity as an engagement signal for your profile's ranking. Responding consistently is both a trust builder and a ranking input.

**Habit 3: Ask for the review, every time.**

Text your happy customer right after delivery or service. Send one link directly to your Google review page, not your homepage, the review page. Two taps and they are done. One review a week is 50 reviews a year. Fifty reviews a year compounds: a higher review count pushes your map ranking up, which brings more clicks, more customers, more reviews. The loop runs itself once it starts.

Most competitors are not doing this. Not because it is hard, but because they keep planning to start. The shop across town that sends the text every single time is quietly widening the gap every week.

## Why this matters more when you are running paid ads

When you run Google Ads, you are paying per click. Every person who clicks your ad and then bounces because your review profile looks thin is a wasted click. Improving your reviews does not just help organic, it directly improves the return on your paid spend by converting more of the traffic you are already paying for.

Think of it this way. Your ad gets the person to the door. Your reviews decide whether they walk in or turn around. Strengthening both sides of that equation is the highest-leverage move a local business can make.

## What this looks like in practice

A meal prep business, or any local service business with repeat customers, has a natural workflow advantage here. Every delivery or order completion is an opportunity to send that text. The timing is built into the business model. Happy customer just received their order. Send the link. Two taps.

The businesses Creekside Marketing works with that grow fastest on paid search are almost always the ones that have also built a consistent review system. The ads bring traffic. The reviews close it. The compounding review count reduces cost-per-acquisition over time because the conversion rate on paid clicks improves as the profile strengthens.

## Staying active on Google Business Profile while running ads

Google Business Profile posts and Google Ads are separate systems, but they both contribute to how you appear in local search. Profile posts signal activity and can appear alongside your map listing. They are free. Most competitors are ignoring them entirely because they posted once and never came back.

Posting weekly, a service highlight, a recent job photo, a short tip, costs nothing and puts you in a category with almost no competition. Your competitors' last post was three years ago. Yours was Tuesday. That is a meaningful signal to Google and a meaningful impression to anyone looking at your profile.

## FAQ

**Do Google reviews help Google Ads performance?**
Indirectly but meaningfully. Reviews do not change your Ad Rank or Quality Score directly. But they improve the conversion rate of paid traffic, because people who click your ad check your profile before calling. A strong review profile means more of those clicks become actual customers.

**How do I get more Google reviews without annoying my customers?**
Ask right after service while the customer is still happy, send one direct link to the review page (not your homepage), keep the message short. The key is the direct link, two taps and done. Customers who had a good experience are typically glad to help when it is this simple.

**Should I focus on ads or reviews first?**
Both, from day one. Start asking for reviews immediately, it costs nothing and the loop starts with your first review. Run ads when your budget allows. The two do not compete for resources. Reviews are a people-and-habit system. Ads are a budget-and-management system. Build them in parallel.

**What should I post on Google Business Profile?**
Anything that signals you are active and doing work: a recent service or delivery, a seasonal menu item, a short tip relevant to your service, a photo from a recent job. The content matters less than the consistency. Once a week keeps you ahead of the overwhelming majority of local competitors.

**How do I respond to a negative Google review?**
Stay calm, be specific, and do not argue. Acknowledge the issue, explain what you did or would do to resolve it, keep it brief. Your response is read by future customers, not just the reviewer. A composed, helpful response to a complaint often does more for your reputation than five additional positive reviews.
