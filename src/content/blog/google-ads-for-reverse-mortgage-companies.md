# Google Ads for Reverse Mortgage Companies: The Full-Funnel Strategy for a 90-Day Sales Cycle

Most Google Ads playbooks break when you apply them to reverse mortgage companies. The sales cycle is too long, the regulatory environment limits your targeting options, and the conversion funnel has four stages before a deal closes. Run these accounts the same way you run a dental practice or a plumber and you will make the wrong optimization decisions based on incomplete data.

We manage Google Ads for a reverse mortgage lender who scaled from $10,000 to $50,000 per month in ad spend. This post is based on a video Peterson published on the Creekside Marketing YouTube channel: [Reverse Mortgage Ads Explained: What Actually Works](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_3QTYR3ful8). It walks through the exact campaign structure we use, why standard tactics fail here, and how to build an account that handles the economics of a $20,000 closed deal.

## Why You Can't Optimize for Closed Deals in Reverse Mortgage Google Ads

Reverse mortgage Google Ads require proxy conversions because the real outcome, a closed deal, happens too far in the future for Google's algorithm to use effectively. A counseling completion can take 90 days to materialize from the first click, sometimes up to six months. You cannot build a smart bidding strategy on signals that arrive that slowly.

The solution is a four-stage conversion hierarchy: all qualified leads, pricing qualified leads, counseling requested, and counseling completed. Counseling completed is the actual closed deal, and it can be worth $20,000 per client. We optimize for pre-qualified leads at the top, specifically phone calls that last over 180 seconds, because those signals come in fast enough to train the algorithm without waiting for the full sales cycle to close.

According to Creekside Marketing's analysis of this account, a target cost per conversion of $50 for pre-qualified leads is sustainable at moderate spend levels. As budget scales toward $100,000 per month, $55 or $60 becomes acceptable. The economics still work at scale when the eventual deal value is $20,000 or more.

Some search terms show over 100% conversion rates. That's not an error. It happens when a single click generates conversions at multiple funnel stages, say a person who becomes both a pre-qualified lead and a pricing qualified lead from the same interaction. Those are your best keywords. Don't pause them.

## The Age Targeting Restriction Nobody Tells You About

The most common mistake in reverse mortgage Google Ads campaigns is trying to narrow your audience by age. You can't. Google restricts demographic targeting in the finance category, which covers reverse mortgages. Turn on age-based audience targeting and your ad delivery drops. This is a Google platform policy, not something you can work around at the audience settings level.

The actual workaround is to use keywords that naturally filter for your audience. Not everyone searches "reverse mortgage" as their first query. Seniors looking for home equity options often search "home equity loan for seniors" without knowing the product name at all. That keyword does the demographic filtering for you at the search intent level, which is more accurate than age-based targeting anyway.

This also means you should never pause keywords just because "reverse mortgage" doesn't appear in the search term. A query like "home equity loan for seniors" is completely relevant to the product even without the explicit phrase. The keyword does the targeting work that audience settings can't do in this category. Don't confuse what the search term says with whether the searcher qualifies.

## The Three-Campaign Structure: Search, PMAX, and Demand Gen

Running Google Ads for reverse mortgage companies at scale requires three campaign types working together, each handling a distinct part of the funnel. Search handles intent. PMAX handles discovery and audience-based reach. Demand Gen handles retargeting. They're not interchangeable. Run them as a coordinated system, not as independent experiments.

**Search** handles your highest-intent queries. We're currently testing broad match against phrase match in this account. Phrase match is outperforming broad match right now based on cost per conversion. We're not making a blanket decision to pause all broad match, though. Every keyword gets evaluated on its own performance data. Some broad match terms convert well. Some don't. The way we approach [keyword match type decisions](/blog/google-ads-keyword-match-types-broad-vs-phrase/) applies here, but with a tighter filter given the cost per click in mortgage and the tight CPA targets.

The most important operational step we take in Search every 30 days: pull the best-performing search terms out of PMAX and push them into dedicated search ad groups. PMAX surfaces the terms but doesn't give you tight control over bidding and budget at the keyword level. Feeding those terms into Search lets you manage them directly. Most agencies skip this step. Don't.

**PMAX** runs two separate campaigns with different audience signals. The first targets people similar to existing counseling completions and counseling requests. These are audiences already deep in the funnel. The ad copy for this group is direct: they know what a reverse mortgage is and they've already started the process somewhere. The second PMAX campaign runs broader messaging for people earlier in their research. Different signals, different copy, different intent level. Mixing both audience types into a single PMAX campaign makes it harder to control spend and harder to understand what's working.

**Demand Gen** is purely for retargeting in this account. We tried a lookalike top-of-funnel campaign in Demand Gen. It didn't work. What works is retargeting people who've already interacted with ads and nudging them further down the funnel. The cost per conversion here is intentionally higher than Search. That's acceptable because we're buying cheap impressions and clicks for warm audiences, keeping the brand in front of people who already know the product exists.

## Device Targeting: Why We Disabled Computers

Computers are disabled in this account. That was a deliberate data-backed decision, not an oversight. The data showed that virtually all conversions come from mobile. Disabling computers freed up budget for the mobile placements that actually produce results. Performance held up after the change.

Here's the thing: the demographic most likely to be researching reverse mortgages often does it on a phone. Desktop intent is lower for this audience and this product type. That's what the conversion data showed, and the targeting reflects it.

Worth revisiting if campaign performance or audience behavior shifts. But don't turn computer targeting back on without a specific data-driven reason.

## CPA Targets: The Rule About When to Stop Pushing Lower

Running target CPA bidding well in a reverse mortgage account means knowing when to stop lowering the target. The rule we follow is simple: if cutting the CPA target causes the campaign to stop spending its full daily budget, you went too far. Raise it back.

Look, the math here isn't complicated. We would rather spend the full $50,000 per month at a $55 CPA than underspend at a $40 CPA. Underspending at this budget level means leaving pipeline volume on the table, and that pipeline eventually produces $20,000 deals. The economics give you room on the cost per lead side. Don't squeeze it so hard you starve the algorithm.

This connects to a longer view of how [bid strategy should evolve as an account matures](/blog/google-ads-bid-strategy-progression-90-day-plan/). The right CPA target at month three looks different than at month eight. Give the algorithm time to learn before tightening the target. Once the account is running well and hitting budget consistently, hold the course rather than keep pushing.

We rotate out the worst-performing headlines occasionally when assets look tired after running hundreds of thousands of impressions. Otherwise, slow and methodical is the pace. Not frequent changes.

## What Not to Do in a Reverse Mortgage Google Ads Account

Running $50,000 per month in reverse mortgage Google Ads demands as much discipline in restraint as in action. This account scaled from $10,000 to $50,000 because we made small, careful changes over time, not because we made big moves. Big moves in a long-cycle account are expensive mistakes that take weeks to identify and correct.

Don't make large CPA adjustments at once. A couple of dollars at a time. Watch spend before adjusting again. Don't block entire days from the ad schedule without recent, specific data showing those days are genuinely underperforming. We ran an ad schedule test in this account several months ago and it tanked performance across the board. We're cautious about revisiting it. Spanning multiple time zones makes the scheduling math even more complicated.

Don't assume a paused PMAX campaign wasn't working. In a 90-day sales cycle, running any campaign for two or three weeks tells you almost nothing about whether the strategy is sound. A pricing-qualified PMAX campaign may have been paused not because the approach was wrong but because the client wasn't willing to wait 90 days for meaningful signal at the spend required to make it work.

Before running any new test, check what's already been tried. A good testing log with outcomes documented is worth more than a new hypothesis. If something was tested in the last six months, there's usually a documented reason it was paused. Running the same test twice wastes budget. Running the same failed test twice wastes trust.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Google Ads for Reverse Mortgage Companies

**Can you target seniors by age in Google Ads for reverse mortgage campaigns?**

No. Google restricts demographic targeting in the finance category, which includes reverse mortgages. Age-based audience targeting isn't available. The workaround is to use keywords that naturally attract your target demographic. Searches like "home equity loan for seniors" filter for your audience through search intent rather than through audience settings, and they work better in practice.

**What's a good cost per lead for reverse mortgage Google Ads?**

Based on Creekside Marketing's analysis, $50 per pre-qualified lead is a reasonable target at moderate spend around $50,000 per month. At higher spend levels approaching $100,000 per month, $55-60 per pre-qualified lead is acceptable because the volume justifies it. These numbers are anchored to a $20,000+ closed deal value at the end of the funnel.

**How long does it take to optimize a reverse mortgage Google Ads account?**

The 60-90 day sales cycle makes early optimization based on closed deals impossible. Build a proxy conversion hierarchy using earlier funnel signals: phone calls over 180 seconds as a qualified lead proxy, pricing conversations as mid-funnel events, counseling completions as the long-term metric. Use those early signals to train bidding algorithms and give campaigns at least 30-45 days before drawing conclusions.

**Should reverse mortgage campaigns use Performance Max or Search?**

Both, for different reasons. Search handles high-intent queries with explicit keyword control and allows direct budget management at the keyword level. PMAX reaches audiences similar to your existing converters and discovers new search terms at scale. The critical operational practice is feeding PMAX's best-performing search terms into dedicated Search ad groups every 30 days so you maintain control over what's actually converting.

**Why do some search terms show over 100% conversion rate?**

In a multi-stage funnel like reverse mortgage, a single person can convert at multiple funnel stages from the same click. Someone might qualify as a pre-qualified lead (phone call over 3 minutes) and then later become a pricing qualified lead in the same 30-day window. Both conversions get credited. This inflates the rate above 100% but the keyword is performing correctly. These are your highest-value keywords.
